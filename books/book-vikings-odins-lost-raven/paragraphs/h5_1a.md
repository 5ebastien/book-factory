The trench cut into the hillside, holding the ship that had carried the lady across the water. Forty people pulled on ropes, their breath rising in white clouds against the pale light. The ship was long and low, its prow curling up into a tight spiral. Every inch of wood was carved with twisting animals, their eyes closed in the frost.

Elsie watched the hull as it slid along the rollers. It was the most beautiful thing she had ever seen. On the deck stood a grave chamber of timber, like a little house waiting for guests. Beds and chests lay on the grass nearby, waiting to go in with her. A carved wagon sat beside them, silent and still. Muninn sat on top of the spiral. He did not move. He would not come down.

The women pulling the ropes stopped, and the ship creaked. Snow melted on the carved animals, running down in thin, dark lines. The lady had died in the winter, and in three days she would be buried. Elsie felt the cold press against her padded coat. It was a quiet kind of cold, deep and heavy. The raven’s head turned slowly, fixing her with one dark eye. He said nothing, but the memory of the winter cold lived in his feathers.

Elsie looked at the ship, then at the raven. The ropes were slack for a moment, held tight by invisible hands. The silence stretched, thin and sharp, until the first man pulled again.
