The steam hung thick in the air, tasting of salt and old smoke. Behind the hall, the kitchen opened like a dark mouth, and in its center stood a cauldron wide as a small pond. Inside, a boar simmered in a brown, rolling broth, its skin pale and bloated. The cook stood by the rim, his apron heavy with wet cloth, and did not look up when Elsie stepped through the fog.

He held a ladle of clear liquid in one hand, the metal cold and bright against the heat. "Drink," he said, and his voice was low, like a stone dropping into water. He did not ask her name, nor did he ask why the raven on her shoulder watched the meat with such quiet attention. Muninn tilted its head, one black eye fixed on the churning water, remembering the smell of the river where it had once slept.

The boar would be eaten tonight, and it would be whole again by morning. The cook said this without sounding surprised, as if speaking of the tide or the changing of seasons. Elsie lifted the bowl, and the warmth spread through her cold fingers, pushing back the chill of the frozen field. The raven ruffled its feathers, and the cook reached for a bunch of onions, weighing them in his palm as he studied the bird.
