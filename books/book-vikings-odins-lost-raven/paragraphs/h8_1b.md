The kitchen was a cave of steam, hidden behind the main hall. Heat pressed against Elsie’s face, thick and wet. In the center stood a cauldron as large as a pond. Inside, a whole boar boiled in bubbling water. The meat was pale and soft, sinking beneath the surface. It was Sæhrímnir, the boar of Valhalla. Every night the warriors ate him to the bones, and every morning he was whole again in the pot, ready to be cooked for the next feast. The cook moved around the rim with quick, sure steps. He did not look surprised by the girl or the raven on her shoulder. His eyes were sharp, narrow, and practical. He watched Muninn with a specific kind of interest. It was the look of a man calculating how the bird would go with onions. Then he turned back to Elsie. He did not ask who she was. He did not ask how she had arrived. He picked up a heavy bowl of clay. It was chipped at the rim and stained with years of grease. He filled it with clear, steaming broth. The liquid shimmered in the dim, hazy light. He handed it to her without a word. Elsie took it. It burned her palms immediately. She curled her fingers around the heat. The cook returned to his cauldron. He stirred the water with a wooden paddle. The steam rose higher, blurring the shape of the pot. It smelled of fat, herbs, and raw iron. Elsie stood still, holding the warm bowl. The boar remained still in the pot, a mass of gray and white meat. The cook kept stirring. The sound of the water boiling was a constant, loud roar. It filled the small, dark space. Elsie looked at the raven. Muninn watched the water. The steam curled around her face.
