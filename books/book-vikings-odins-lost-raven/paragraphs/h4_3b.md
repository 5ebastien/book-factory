The fog rolled in on the second day, thick and white, until the sea and the sky became one thing. The wind had dropped, leaving a silence that pressed against the hull like a cold hand. Spray froze on the rigging, and the cold was sharp enough to bite through Elsie’s padded coat. She stood at the prow, the sunstone warm against her chest, while the world outside dissolved into grey nothing.

Muninn sat on the mast, his feathers ruffled, watching the void with one bright eye. Ketil shivered beside the steering oar, his knuckles white, muttering about home where the fjord was never this wide or this empty. Old Grim held the tiller, his face carved from weather, waiting for a sign that had not come. Elsie lifted the stone from her neck, holding it up against the blank white.

There, inside the milky haze, a single point of light burned. It was small and steady, a pinprick of gold in the endless fog. She pointed to it, her finger trembling slightly in the cold air. Grim did not hesitate. He laughed, a dry, sharp sound, and turned the heavy oar by her direction. The ship groaned as it shifted course, cutting through the mist.

"Some talk of stones like this," Grim said, his voice low, "but no man has ever proved it."

The light stayed with them, a private secret, until the fog thinned at last. Then the mountains rose from the grey, dark and jagged, waiting in the distance.
