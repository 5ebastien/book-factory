The bridge appeared where the mist thinned, a narrow span of something like light and something like ice. Three colours shifted across its surface, pale and strange, until the path seemed solid enough to hold. Elsie lifted her foot, and the trainers touched the surface with a sharp ring, like a glass bell. The sound faded quickly, leaving a cold hum in the air. She took another step, and the light beneath her bent, then straightened again.

At the far end, a man stood watching her. He wore a wide hat, and a horn hung at his belt, polished and dark. His eyes were sharp, moving from the stone round her neck to the ravens perched on her shoulders. He did not stop her, though the bridge groaned faintly under her weight. He looked at the sunstone, then at Muninn, and finally at Huginn, who watched with tilted eyes.

Muninn shivered, a ripple of memory passing through his feathers. The man’s gaze held, steady and quiet, while the bridge rang softly with every breath of wind. Elsie moved forward, one step at a time, until she stood before him. He nodded once, a small motion, and stepped aside. The path ahead opened, dark and silent, waiting for the next step.
