The bridge was made of something like light and something like ice. It shimmered in three colours that did not have names. Elsie stepped onto it and felt the weight of her trainers sink slightly. It rang under her feet like a thin pane of glass. She held her breath, waiting for it to shatter. It did not. The surface held firm beneath her soles. At the far end, a man stood watching her. He was tall and still, with a large horn hanging at his belt. He looked at her the way a farmer watches the grass grow. He could hear it grow, Elsie knew, though she did not say it. He did not raise a hand to stop her. His eyes moved to the sunstone at her neck. Then they shifted to the two ravens perched on her shoulders. Muninn ruffled its feathers, remembering a storm from a thousand years ago. Huginn turned its head, waiting for a question that would never come. The man’s gaze lingered on the stone. He nodded once, a slow motion that felt heavy in the air. He stepped aside, making room for her to pass. Elsie walked forward, one foot in front of the other. The bridge sang a low note under her weight. She kept her eyes on the man’s boots. They were old leather, worn smooth by years of standing. The wind from the left pushed against her hood. She felt the cold bite through her jacket. The man watched her go. His face did not change. He did not speak. He did not move from his spot. Elsie passed him, her heart hammering against her ribs. The air behind her smelled of ozone and wet earth. She did not turn around. The bridge continued ahead, shimmering and silent. Her next step landed on the solid ground at the end.
