The new skates squeaked against Ketil's old leather boots. Ketil stood up, his face pale under a smudge of dirt. Elsie watched him struggle with the iron-tipped stick. He pushed off and slid three feet before losing his balance. He crashed into the muddy edge of the ice. Elsie glided past him without breaking stride. She kept her arms close to her sides. The bone blades cut clean lines in the grey frost. Other children slipped and slid around them, shrieking with cold air. Some wore thick wool caps. Others had only bandages wrapped tight over their ears. The sun hung low and white in the distance. It did not seem warm at all. Ketil picked himself up, brushing mud from his tunic. He pointed a stiff finger toward the open water. "At home, the ice never leaves," he said. "It stays in the shadows of the fjord until summer breaks." Elsie nodded, thinking of the sea he left behind. "My mother watches for me," he continued. "She looks at the waves for the shape of the Sea-Hart." Elsie felt a strange pull in her chest at the mention of the ship. She shifted her weight, testing the grip of her blades. The marsh air smelled of wet silt and old pine. Ketil sat down on the frozen bank, breathing hard. He wrapped his hands around his knees. Elsie remained standing, swaying slightly on the thin ice. The crowd around them thinned as the morning waned. No one looked at the raven perched nearby. They only saw a boy and a girl skating. Ketil kicked at a patch of dead grass. "It is quiet there," he said softly. "Too quiet without the wind." Elsie looked at the water, dark and still. She did not answer. The ice groaned faintly under her weight. She held perfectly still, listening to the silence spread. Ketil leaned forward, his breath fogging in the chill.
