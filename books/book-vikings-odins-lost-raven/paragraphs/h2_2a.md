The ice on the Foss marsh was thin and grey at the edges, and the air smelled of wet mud and old fish. Elsie adjusted the bone skates until they fit her trainers, then pushed off with an iron-tipped stick. Ketil watched her go, his own stick clattering against the frozen surface as he tried to keep up. He fell twice, skidding into the slushy grass, but he laughed as he brushed the wet mud from his knees. The children were scattered across the marsh, their voices thin and far away in the cold morning. Muninn stood on the bank, preening a wing that looked too dark for the light.

Elsie glided closer to the center of the ice, where the surface was smooth and hard. She could see the black water moving slowly beneath the thin layer, like a secret kept just out of reach. Ketil called out to her, his voice flat and quiet, and she turned back to see him sitting on the bank. He told her about the fjords back home, where the ice stayed in the shadows until the summer came. He said his mother would be watching the sea for him, waiting for the ship to return.

Elsie sat down beside him, pulling her coat tighter around her shoulders. The wind moved through the reeds, bending them low and still. She looked at the stick in her hand, the iron tip dark and dull. Ketil looked at the water, his face serious and calm. The ice cracked softly under their weight, a small sound that did not go away.
