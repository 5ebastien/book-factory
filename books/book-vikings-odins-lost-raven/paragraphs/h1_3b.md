The raven sat on the sign of the Jorvik Viking Centre, still as a statue. Elsie held the stone up to the queue, and the people simply weren't there. A woman in a wool dress carried a bucket through mud, her boots sinking with a wet thud. A man at the end of the row turned a wooden cup on a lathe. The wood shavings curled up in long, pale ribbons. The street had kept its name, she realised. Coppergate was the street of the cup-makers. The modern shop fronts faded like smoke in wind. The sound of traffic vanished, replaced by the smell of wood smoke and wet earth.

Under her feet, the pavement grew soft and uneven. The layer of glass and steel thinned, and something older rose up from below. The museum floor was no longer glass; it was packed earth and broken timber. Elsie looked down at the street that had been there in the seventies. The past was not a picture in a book. It was here, warm and breathing, while the present slipped away from her hands. Muninn watched her with one dark eye, silent and still. The raven did not speak, but its presence felt heavy, like a weight on the air. The wet mud under the wooden cobbles steamed faintly in the cold.
