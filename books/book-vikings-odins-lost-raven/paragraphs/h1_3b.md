The queue for the Jorvik Viking Centre snaked around the corner, impatient and cold. Muninn stood on the sign, a black comma against the grey stone. Elsie pulled the sunstone from her pocket. The glass was warm, not with heat, but with weight. She held it up. The line of people dissolved like sugar in hot water. A wet smell rose, thick and green, like turned earth. The asphalt beneath her boots softened. It was no longer concrete. It was packed mud. A woman walked past, close enough to touch. She wore a wool dress, dark with age and rain. In her hands, she carried a wooden bucket that tapped against her ankle. The clack was loud in the sudden silence. Elsie looked down the street. The modern shopfronts were gone. Timber frames leaned against each other. Smoke curled from chinks in the walls. A man stood at his doorway. He held a wooden cup in one hand and a tool in the other. He spun the cup on a lathe. The wood turned slowly, a pale ring of dust falling to the ground. The cup was not finished. It was thin and trembling under his thumb. The smell of wet wood filled Elsie’s nose. It was sharp and alive. She watched the man work. His face was hidden in shadow, but his hands were steady. The stone in her neck grew heavier. The street was not a museum. It was a place. The mud squelched softly around her shoes. The woman with the bucket walked away. The man did not look up. He kept turning. The wood peeled away in a long, continuous ribbon. Elsie’s breath misted in the air. It hung there, a ghost of her own body. The street of the cup-makers was waiting.
