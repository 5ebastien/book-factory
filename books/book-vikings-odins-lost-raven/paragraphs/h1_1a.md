The finds officer was out until four, so they waited in the Viking gallery. The room was quiet and smelled of polished wood and old dust. They stood in front of the large glass case holding the Vale of York Hoard. Grandad leaned against the wall and started talking. He spoke as if he had been there, on that January morning near Harrogate. He described the father and son with their metal detectors. He mentioned the cold ground and the sudden beep of the machines. Then the careful brushes and the surprising shine in the frozen earth. A gilded silver cup, full of coins.

Elsie looked at the cup through the glass. It sat in the middle of the case, bright and still. It held six hundred and seventeen coins. One of them had come from Samarkand, Grandad said. That was a very long way for a small piece of metal. She reached up and touched the sunstone around her neck. The stone was warm against her collar. She pressed her hand over it. For a second, the world blurred. The cup was not behind glass. It was new. It was bright. It was full and heavy, right in front of her eyes. The coins jingled softly in the still air.

Then the moment snapped. The case was just a case again. The room was just a room. Elsie stepped back. Her heart was beating fast. Behind her, something tapped on the gallery window. *Tap. Tap. Tap.* It was a sharp, rhythmic sound. Elsie turned around. On the sill outside the large window, a raven waited. It was black and still. One eye caught the dim light of the museum. It looked directly at her. Elsie did not move. The raven did not move. The cold air from the window pushed a strand of hair across her face. She watched the bird. The bird watched her.
