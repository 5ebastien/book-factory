The finds officer was out until four, so they waited in the Viking gallery. The room was quiet, save for the hum of the lights. Grandad stood by the glass case, where the Vale of York Hoard lay in the dark. He told its story as if he had been there. A father and a son with detectors, on a cold January day near Harrogate. They had found a silver cup, gilded and bright. Inside it sat six hundred and seventeen coins, one from Samarkand. Elsie looked at the cup through the stone around her neck. For a second, the metal was new and bright and full. Then it faded back to old silver, cold and still. Behind her, something tapped on the gallery window.

The sound was small, like a fingernail on glass.

Elsie turned, and the raven was on the sill outside. It waited there, head tilted, eyes black and deep. The gallery floor was polished wood, reflecting the dull light above. Other visitors stood by the next case, reading labels, but nobody looked at the window. Elsie felt the stone pull against her throat, a heavy warmth. The raven tapped again, once, and then sat very still. Grandad was still talking, his voice low and steady. He spoke of the coins, of the journey they had taken. Elsie kept looking at the bird, while the wind pressed against the glass. The tap echoed in the quiet air. It was waiting for her to move.
