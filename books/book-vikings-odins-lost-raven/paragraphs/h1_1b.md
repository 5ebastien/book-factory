The river Ouse ran high and brown below the path, and the cold air smelled of wet earth and winter. The man in the wide hat walked beside her, his boots making soft, heavy sounds on the gravel. He spoke about names, saying that York was full of words carried across the sea by long ago. A -by, he said, was a farm, and a -thorpe was a farm further out.

Elsie thought of her own name, and the stone hung warm against her neck under her coat. He told her that her surname was a farm, too, and he seemed to find that very funny. His one eye crinkled at the corner, but he did not smile with his mouth. The path was empty except for the sound of the water lapping at the bank.

"What would you give," he asked, "to get back something you had lost?"

Elsie was still thinking about it when the silence around them changed. She looked to her left, and the man in the wide hat was gone. On the path where he had been standing, the raven perched in the freezing air. It watched her with a stillness that felt older than the stone at her throat.
