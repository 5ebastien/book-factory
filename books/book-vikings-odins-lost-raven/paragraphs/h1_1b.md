The man matched her stride along the riverside path, the wide brim of his hat tilting against the pale winter sky. He spoke about the words stuck in the city’s mouth, names that had settled into the stones. "You hear it everywhere," he said, nodding toward the high, brown water of the Ouse. "A -by. That was a farm, long ago. A place to keep sheep and store grain." He tapped the muddy ground with his boot. "And a -thorpe? Further out. The farm you sent your cousin to because you didn't want him stealing your chickens." Elsie pulled the sandwich bag tighter against her chest. The sunstone pressed a dull weight against her collarbone, a reminder of things lent, not found. "My name is a farm," she said, testing the words. They felt too big for her mouth, like a coat two sizes too large. The man stopped walking. He did not look at her, but his one dark eye seemed to turn. "It is," he said. "Tell me. If you lost something important. Not a key. Not a book. Something that mattered. What would you give to get it back?" The question hung in the cold air, heavy and wet. Elsie opened her mouth to answer, but the wind gusted, whipping her jacket around her legs. The brown water rushed against the stone walls, churning up silt. She was still turning the thought over when the path ahead cleared. The man in the wide hat was gone. No footprints in the mud marked his departure. Only the raven remained, standing on the dead branch of a willow. Its black feathers stood slightly on end. It stared at her with a beady, ancient eye. The wind stopped. The river lapped. She stood very still, her hands cold.
