The house sat above the water like a brown lump on a grey hill.
It was long and low, with a roof of packed turf.
Goats stood on the roof, chewing the dried grass and moss.
Ketil’s mother came out of the dark door running.
Her boots thudded on the stone steps.
She grabbed Ketil and held him tight.
She did not let go for a very long time.
Elsie waited by the door and watched the woman’s face.
It was hard and fast and full of something Elsie did not know the name of.
After a minute, Elsie turned her back.
She walked to the wall of the house.
The goats on the roof were close enough to touch.
One of them chewed a twig and looked down at her.
Its eyes were dark and flat.
Elsie scratched her nose and looked at the fjord.
The ice was breaking up in the shadows.
That night, the air in the house was thick with smoke.
A fire burned in the middle of the long room.
Everyone sat close to the heat.
They talked about only one thing.
The great lady of the fjord had died in the winter.
Her body was cold in the snow.
Her ship was being pulled up from the water.
In three days, she would be buried in it.
They would put everything she needed in the ship.
Cups and clothes and armor.
Muninn perched on the rafters above the fire.
The raven did not sleep.
It watched the faces of the people by the light.
The wood creaked under its feet.
Elsie watched the raven, and the raven watched the fire.
