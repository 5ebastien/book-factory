Ketil’s mother ran from the turf-roofed house with such speed that the air seemed to shatter around her. She held Ketil so tightly that his ribs creaked, and the woman did not let go for a long time. Elsie stepped away from the pair, drawing closer to the edge of the roof where the goats stood. The animals chewed on the frozen moss, ignoring the screaming woman below, their eyes dull and flat. Up here, the wind was sharp and clean, carrying the smell of wet stone and dying ice.

By night, the fire in the center of the long house had burned low. The smoke rose in thin, gray ribbons, twisting against the dark timber beams above. Everyone in the room spoke in low voices, their words tangled together like wet wool. They all said the same thing, over and over, until the repetition became a kind of music. The great lady of the fjord had died in the winter, and her body waited for the thaw. Her ship was being hauled up the slope, its black hull scraping against the snow.

In three days, she would be buried in it. They would place everything she would need into the hold, from food to tools, all of it packed tight. Elsie watched the flames dance on the soot-blackened walls, while Ketil sat beside her, his face pale and strange. He did not look at her, but his eyes remained fixed on the door, as if expecting it to open. On the rafters directly above Elsie’s head, Muninn pressed his beak into his wing. The raven did not sleep, his one visible eye open and reflecting the firelight.
