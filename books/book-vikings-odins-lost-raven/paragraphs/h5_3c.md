The cold pressed down on the hilltop once the crowd had gone.
Elsie stood alone, her breath misting in the starlight.
The sunstone hung warm against her chest, a steady pulse.
Through the stone, the buried ship sat in its trench.
But as the light failed, the image changed.
Water filled the dry ditch, dark and moving.
The sky above the ship was not this sky.
It was a deep, strange blue, wrong for the night.
Far off, something huge and still waited in the distance.
It looked like a tree, but far too big to be one.
Muninn paced the rail of the ship, quick and sharp.
He spoke memories faster than she could catch them.
Faces blurred into sounds, sounds into names.
She could not follow the rush of it.
Then, a shadow moved on the path below.
Ketil climbed the slope, his breath coming hard.
He stopped beside her, hugging his shoulders.
"I am not leaving you up here," he said.
His voice was low, tight with worry.
Elsie watched the shifting water through the stone.
The giant tree seemed to sway in the wrong sky.
Muninn turned his head, one eye bright and alert.
He looked at Ketil, then back at the water.
Ketil stared at the raven, his face pale in the dark.
He did not move away, though his hand tightened on his coat.
The cold bit through Elsie’s jeans and into her legs.
She kept her eyes on the strange, moving scene.
