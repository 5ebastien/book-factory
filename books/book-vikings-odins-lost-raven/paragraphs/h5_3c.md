The cold settled on the hill as the stars came out one by one, until the fjord below was a sheet of dark glass. Elsie stood near the ship’s rail, her breath thin in the air, and watched the water ripple without a wind. Ketil climbed the slope again because he would not leave her alone with the stone, his footsteps crunching softly on the frozen grass. He stopped beside her and looked at the grave, his face tight in the pale starlight. Through the sunstone, the ship was no longer buried; it sat on deep, moving water beneath a sky that held too many lights. Far off, a shape rose from the horizon like a tree, though it had no leaves and no bark. Muninn paced the wooden rail, faster and faster, his head turning as if counting memories that no longer fit in his mind. The raven stopped when the tree moved a single branch, though the wind was still. Ketil said he did not like it, and he was not leaving, so he pulled his coat tighter against the chill. Elsie reached out her hand toward the moving tree, and the light from the stone pulsed once against her palm.
