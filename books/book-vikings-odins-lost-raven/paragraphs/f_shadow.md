Elsie crouched low, the stone cold and hard in her fist, while the air grew still around her. Between the two sleeping wolves, the shadows were thick and smelled of iron and old smoke. She waited, her breath held tight, as the firelight flickered against the stone walls. Then, a soft thud sounded from the corridor, slow and heavy, like a footstep on wood. Sköll came round the corner of the high seat, his grey fur dull in the dim light. He did not look at Elsie; his head was turned toward the stone, following the scent of the sun. The large wolf paused, his yellow eyes fixed on her hand, and the silence stretched out between them. Elsie stepped forward, opening her coat to show the raven, but the stone remained hidden in her grip.

Sköll lowered his head, and the noise in the hall seemed to vanish completely.

The one-eyed man stirred on the throne, but did not open his eyes, while the raven inside Elsie’s coat went very still. The wolf’s nose twitched, catching the air, and he took one step closer, his claws pressing into the floor. Elsie did not move back, because she knew that nothing worth having was free. She stood there, the shadow swallowing her feet, while the wolf watched the fist that held the light.
