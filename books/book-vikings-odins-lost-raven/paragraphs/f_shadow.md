The stone in her fist was cold, a heavy rock of ice and stone that burned her palm. Elsie pressed her back against the rough wood of the high seat, holding her breath. The silence in the hall was thick, broken only by the slow, wet breathing of the wolves. They lay coiled around her, their fur matted and grey, blocking the exit to the main hall. Sköll’s head rounded the corner of the seat, moving with a slow, terrifying certainty. His eyes were not eyes, but pits of black hunger that tracked the scent of the sun.

Elsie stepped out into the open space, spreading her arms to face the beast. She held the stone tight against her hip, the weight of it an anchor in the dark. Muninn shifted inside her jacket, a soft rustle of feathers against her ribs. The raven spoke only in memories, but Elsie could feel the weight of them pressing against her spine. Sköll did not growl. He did not snarl. He simply looked at the stone, and then he looked at Elsie, his lip curling to show teeth like broken knives.

"You want this," Elsie said, her voice shaking only a little. She did not offer it. She stood her ground, her trainers flat on the cold floor, her knuckles white around the sunstone. Sköll took one step forward, his claws clicking on the stone floor. The fires along the walls bent away from him, their flames turning blue and cold. Elsie felt the pull of the stone, a strong tug in her hand that tried to draw it closer to her heart. She knew nothing worth having was free, and she knew the wolf would hunt the light until the end of time. The beast stopped, ten paces away, its breath a visible cloud in the cold air. The choice hung between them, heavy and silent, suspended in the dark.
