The valley was long, and it was dark.
Round by the hills, the road had gone quiet for a very long time.
Muninn remembered it green, full of people and the smell of wet grass.
The raven did not speak, but its eyes held the past in them.
Elsie walked slowly, her trainers crunching on the old stones.
In the memory, a master rode down the path on an eight-legged horse.
He was laughing, his beard flying in the cold wind, and the horse’s hooves rang like metal.
It remembered Huginn then, young and small, perched on his shoulder.
Now the road stretched on, empty and black, with no sound of footsteps.
Nothing followed them down the dark path, and the silence was heavy.
The cold air pressed against Elsie’s coat, smelling of moss and time.
She began to understand why the raven had been missed so badly.
The dark gathered around them, thick and soft as wool.
Muninn’s wing touched her sleeve, a brush of feathers in the dimness.
The end of the path was only a shadow ahead, waiting.
