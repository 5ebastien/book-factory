The path wound down through a gap in the hills. The valley below was dark and very old. Elsie’s trainers crunched on the frost-hardened dirt. Muninn sat on her shoulder, his feathers flat against the wind. He did not look forward. He looked back. Memories spilled out of him like warm water. He showed her the valley green and full of people. Children ran between the feet of horses. Smoke rose from thatched roofs. It smelled of pine needles and wet wool. Then the memory shifted. A massive figure rode down the slope on a horse with eight legs. The beast’s hooves did not make a sound. The man in the wide hat laughed, a sound like cracking ice. His hand rested gently on the raven’s back. The joy in that memory was sharp and bright. It made Elsie’s chest ache. Muninn shifted his weight. Another memory surfaced, softer this time. A young raven with bright, curious eyes. It was Huginn, before he was lost to questions. He chirped, asking where the wind would go next. The two birds were close then, head to head. Now they were torn apart by eleven centuries of silence. The road stretched on, long and empty. No wolf ran in the shadows behind them. No one called out. Elsie felt a strange, heavy sadness settle in her bones. She understood, finally, why the world felt so empty without him. It was not just a pet missing. It was half of a mind gone. The valley grew darker as the trees thickened. The air grew colder. She pulled her collar up. Muninn closed his eyes. The last image in his head was of a table set for dinner, with a place left empty for the one who was not there.
