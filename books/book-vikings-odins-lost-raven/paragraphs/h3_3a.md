The staithe was a wide slab of stone leading down to the water. Bard’s crew sat on the steps, their backs against the damp wall. One man held a strip of cloth over his eye. He dabbed at the blood with a thumb that looked too large for his face. Ketil stood near the bottom step, his hands shoved deep into his pockets. He looked like he wanted to be anywhere else in the world.

Elsie climbed down to join them. The air smelled of wet wool and old rope. Muninn perched on her shoulder, his feathers ruffled by the breeze. The raven said nothing, which was rare for him, but the silence felt heavy. Bard stood apart from the group, leaning on a wooden stake driven into the stone. He watched the water line, checking the depth of the tide.

"The Northumbrians are in the town," Bard said. His voice was flat, like he was reading a list of supplies. "They will not get out again. The army is too strong for them." He spat into the water. It made a small, dark ripple.

Elsie waited for him to care about who won. He did not care. He only wanted his ship in the water. He wanted the town behind him by noon. He looked at the crew, counting heads. He wanted every pair of hands he could find.

Ketil shifted his weight from one foot to the other. He looked at Elsie, then at Bard, then at the raven. His face was pale. The smell of the harbor was thick in his nostrils, mixing with the metallic tang of the cut on the man's face. Bard reached down and pulled the stake free. It left a dark hole in the stone. He turned back to the water, his broad shoulders blocking the view of the sea.
