Bard sat on the lowest step of the staithe, his back against the cold stone. The other men huddled nearby, sharing the silence after the noise. One had a cut over his eye, red and wet, and he dabbed it with his sleeve again and again. The blood stained the wool, but he did not look up at anyone. Bard watched the water where the Sea-Hart’s ropes were tied, and he watched the town behind him. The Northumbrians were in the streets, he said, their voices rising in the air. They would not get out again, he said, because the army was too strong for them. It did not matter to him who won the fight. He wanted his ship in the water and the town behind him by noon. He wanted every pair of hands he could get.

Elsie stood on the steps above him, holding her coat tighter. Muninn sat on the railing, a dark shape against the grey morning. Ketil sat beside the bleeding man, looking at his hands as if they belonged to someone else. The staithe was quiet now, the shouting gone into the town. Bard looked up at Elsie, then at the water, then at the sky. He tapped his foot on the stone, a slow, steady rhythm. The crowd in the market was thinning, people moving away from the staithe. Bard pointed toward the harbor, where the tide was turning. He needed hands, and he needed them now.
