The branch was high up the Tree, far above the floor. It jutted out into the dark space where the leaves turned slow and heavy. On one side of the limb, the bark was worn smooth in a tight ring. It looked like a rope had hung there for a long time. Below that smooth circle was an old, jagged scar. A spear had gone into the wood there, ages ago. The split was dry and pale against the dark bark. Muninn shifted on the tip of the branch. The raven’s feathers ruffled, though the air was still.

Then Muninn remembered it out loud.

It was not a sound. It was a weight in Elsie’s head. Nine nights its master had hung there. He was hurt and hungry and thirsty. The rope bit into his shoulders. The cold wind tore at his coat. He had no food. He had no water. He hung there to learn the runes. On the ninth night, he came down knowing them. The memory faded from her mind like steam.

Elsie looked at the scar again. It was just a mark in the wood. She reached out her hand. Her fingers were cold from the air under the Tree. She pressed her palm flat against the rough bark. The wood was warm. It was not warm from the sun. It was warm like a stone held in a pocket for a while. The heat seeped into her skin. She did not pull her hand away. The warmth was steady and real. The scar was a wound that had not healed. It was a place where something had been left behind. The raven watched her from the high branch. Its eye was black and still. Elsie kept her hand on the warm wood.
