Elsie stood before the thick branch where the wood was old and silent. The bark there was worn smooth in a tight ring, as if a rope had hung from it for years. Below that smooth circle, a deep scar split the wood, dark and jagged. It looked like an old wound that had never fully healed. Muninn sat on the lower twig, his eyes closed, but his head began to turn. The air around them grew cold, settling into the hollow of the branch. The raven opened his beak and spoke without moving his legs. He remembered nine nights in the dark, with no food and no water. He remembered the pain of the spear in his side and the thirst in his throat. His master had hung there to learn the runes, hurt and hungry. On the ninth night, he had come down knowing them all. Elsie reached out and placed her hand on the scar. The wood was warm under her fingers, a steady heat against the winter chill. She did not pull her hand away.
