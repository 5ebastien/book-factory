On the first clear night after the storm, the sea was still heaving, but slowly now. The crew lay in heaps between the sea chests under their cloaks, and Ketil was asleep in the bow with his mouth open. Elsie stood in the stern with old Grim. He had the steering oar under his arm, a great blade of oak on the right-hand side of the ship, and he did not seem to do anything with it. He only stood; now and then he looked up at the stars, and now and then he shifted his feet, and the Sea-Hart went where he wanted.

"How do you know where we are?" said Elsie.

"Stars," said Grim. "And the swell. It comes from the same way for days. You feel it in your feet."

He had been doing it for fifty years, he said, and he had never once been lost, not for long. Muninn was inside her coat, warm against her, while Elsie kept looking back at the pale line of the wake that ran away behind them into the dark. It felt as though something were following it. Grim noticed, because he noticed everything for somebody who never seemed to look.

He put his free hand inside the neck of his tunic and pulled out a thong with something small and silver on the end of it. It was a hammer no longer than her little finger, worn so smooth that its edges had gone soft. He lifted it over his head and hung it round her neck, next to the stone.

"Thor keeps things off," he said. "The giants. The sea-snake. Whatever that is." He nodded at the wake. "That's what it's for."

Then he looked back up at the stars. Off to the left, where he said the whales came up before dawn, the dark water was very still; in the bow, Ketil turned over and muttered in his sleep.
