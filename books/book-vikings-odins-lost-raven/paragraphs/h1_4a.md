The cold hit her skin before the smell did, a sharp bite of frost melting into wet wood. She stood in a lane of mud between houses of woven wattle, the stone cold against her neck. The air was thick with smoke, pork fat, and the damp earth rising from the ground. It was morning here, a raw spring morning in Jorvik. York had gone, and Grandad with it, and the dusk she remembered.

A woman with a bucket stopped and stared at Elsie’s jeans. Her eyes moved from the denim to the trainers, wide and unsure. The town was the army’s now, the woman said, her voice low. The Great Army from over the sea had taken it last November. Elsie understood every word, the old words sitting heavy in her chest.

The raven dropped onto her shoulder as if it had always sat there. Its feathers were black and dry, distinct against her padded coat. It spoke in memories, not questions. The woman’s face had crumbled two days ago. The pig had died in the ditch. The silence followed.

Elsie wanted to run back to the frost, to the bench, to the present. The stone burned lightly. She must go to the high hall. Muninn knew the way. The raven knew.
