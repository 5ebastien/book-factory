The hall stretched before her, a cavern of stone and shadow that seemed to swallow sound. Elsie walked the center line, the sunstone tight in her fist. It pulsed with a warm, amber light that cut through the gloom. Faces lined the walls, hundreds of them, still as painted portraits. They did not cheer. They did not block her path. They simply watched, their eyes fixed on the small sun in her hand. Behind her, the floorboards creaked under a weight far greater than a human could bear. Sköll did not run. He walked with a slow, rolling gait, his grey fur dull against the dark wood. The distance between them did not shrink, but the air grew colder with every step. Elsie’s breath misted in front of her, hanging in the stillness. She kept her gaze straight ahead, toward the far end of the long hall. The stone felt heavy, a dense ball of heat that seeped into her palm. She could feel the wolf’s presence behind her, a silent pressure against her back. The einherjar remained silent, their armor clinking softly as they shifted their weight. No one moved to intercept the beast. No one tried to pull Elsie aside. She counted her steps, one by one, keeping her rhythm steady. The light from the stone flickered, casting long, wavering shadows across the floor. Sköll’s low growl was the only sound that filled the vast space. It was a deep, rumbling note that vibrated in her chest. Elsie’s fingers tightened around the stone, her knuckles white. The end of the hall was still far away, a dark rectangle in the distance. She did not speed up. She did not slow down. She simply walked, carrying the light forward.
