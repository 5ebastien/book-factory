The floor of the hall was wide and cold under her trainers. She walked down the middle line, and the stone in her fist hummed with a low, warm light. It was the only sun in the room, a small and steady glow against the dark wood. Every face in the hall turned toward her, and none of them moved to block the path. The eyes of the warriors were still and watchful, like stones in a garden.

Behind her, Sköll came down the floor without hurrying. The wolf moved with a slow and heavy grace, and the sound of his claws was soft on the wood. Nobody stopped him, and nobody stopped her either. The air grew thick and silent, until the only sound was the deep thud of her heart. Elsie did not look back once, although she knew the shadow was right behind her.

Muninn perched on her shoulder, his feathers ruffled and still. The raven said nothing, but his memory of the cold field played in Elsie's mind. She saw the frost and the quiet earth, and then the hall again. The walk felt very long, and the end of the hall felt very far.

The fires on the walls bent away from the wolf as he passed them. They did not go out, but they shrank back into the stone. The smell of smoke and old metal filled the narrow space between the pillars. Elsie kept her eyes on the far door, where the light was dim and strange.

She took one step, and then another, until the floor seemed to end. The wolf stopped moving, and he watched her with two bright and waiting eyes. Elsie held the stone up, and the small sun in her hand began to fade.
