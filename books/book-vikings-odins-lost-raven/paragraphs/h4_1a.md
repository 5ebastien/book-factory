The North Sea was not water. It was a grey, heaving mass that filled the entire sky. The Sea-Hart climbed the crest of a wave as high as a house. Elsie held her breath as the boat slid down the other side. The oak planks bent and creaked, a sound like a living thing stretching in its sleep. Bard said that was exactly what kept the ship from breaking. He stood near the stern, his hands white-knuckled on the rail. Nobody spoke. There was only the wind, a constant shriek that tore through the rigging. Elsie bailed water with a wooden scoop. Her arms burned, then ached, then went numb. She could no longer feel her fingers. Ketil was three rows down, bailing just as hard. His face was pale under the brim of his hood. Every splash hit the deck with a cold slap. The spray froze on Elsie’s cheeks before she could wipe it away. Muninn rode inside her coat, a warm, hard lump against her ribs. The raven did not cry out. He was too busy remembering. In the dark, she heard him whisper. It was the memory of a ship that had not come home. The words were soft, drowned by the thunder of the sea. Elsie’s scoop dipped into the black water. She pulled it up, heavy and full.
