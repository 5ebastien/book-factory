The wind did not stop; it changed. For two days, the Sea-Hart climbed high and slid down, while the oak planks bent and creaked like something alive. Bard said the ship kept its shape because it breathed with the water. Everybody bailed, and Elsie held the wooden scoop until her arms stopped feeling. The spray was cold beyond anything she had known in York, and it stung her face while the dark pressed in around the mast. Muninn rode inside her coat, warm against her back, and she felt the raven remember very quietly. A ship had sailed from the fjord and had not come home, Muninn told her, though the words were only a cold memory of wood and salt. Elsie did not ask where that ship was, or who had been on it; she only kept scooping the green water from the deck. The storm roared above them, a low and endless sound that shook the oars and the ropes, and the grey sky hung low like a wet wool blanket. She looked at Bard, who stood firm at the helm, and he did not look back. The bucket tipped in her hands, sending a splash of seawater over the gunwale, and the ship groaned deep in its bones.
