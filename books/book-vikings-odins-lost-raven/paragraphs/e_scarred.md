Sköll opened his jaws for the stone.

Elsie had three things to meet him with. They were enough to finish it, just, and not enough to finish it whole. The wolf's teeth closed on air. He backed down the hall with his ears flat, and went out of the west door with nothing, after the sun, where he belonged.

Muninn walked up her arm and across onto the one-eyed man's shoulder. It remembered a frozen field, out loud, and then it was home, and quiet.

The one-eyed man took off his wide hat, and Elsie knew him. Odin.

"It is not finished," he said. "The wolf will remember the way here now. Somebody has to pay for that." He touched the place where his own eye had been. "I paid for what I wanted, once. Nothing worth having is free."

"What do you want?"

"One memory," said Odin. "I will choose it. You will not miss it much."

Elsie thought about it for a long time. Then she nodded.

He put two fingers on her forehead, very lightly, the way Grandad did when he was checking for a temperature.

She was in York at dusk, on the same Monday, with the stone round her neck and the traffic going past. Two weeks later the stone went into a glass case in the Yorkshire Museum, with her name on the label.

She went to see it on a Saturday. She stood in front of the case for a long time. Something was missing, and she could not have said what. There was a gap somewhere, about the size of one afternoon, and she did not know what had been in it. She only knew that it had been a good one.
