Sköll opened his jaws for the stone.

Elsie had nothing to meet him with, because she had carried nothing the whole way. All she had was Muninn and the stone and her own two hands, and she used all of it. She threw Muninn up into the air towards the one-eyed man, as hard as she could. The raven's wings caught, and it flew the last few yards on its own until it landed on its master's shoulder, home.

Then Sköll's jaws closed, and the stone was gone out of her hand.

He swallowed it. For a moment the wolf's grey throat glowed from the inside, like a lamp behind a curtain; then he turned and went out of the west door, slowly, with the light inside him. Nobody in the hall said anything.

The one-eyed man took off his wide hat, and Elsie knew him. Odin.

"You brought him home," said Odin, "with nothing. I did not think anybody could." He looked at the west door for a long time. "Nothing worth having is free. You have paid, and now everybody will. Go home, Elsie."

She was in York at dusk, on the same Monday, with nothing round her neck.

In the morning the sun rose weak and small, and by lunchtime York was sunk in a brown half-light, like evening in the middle of the day. The streetlights came on by themselves while people stood in the road and looked up. On the news they said it was dust, or smoke from somewhere far away; nobody could explain it. Elsie walked home through it with her hands in her sleeves.

She knew.
