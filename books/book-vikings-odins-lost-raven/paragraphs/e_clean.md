Sköll opened his jaws for the stone.

Elsie had four things to meet him with, and every one of them did what it could. It was one short, and she felt the gap where the last one should have been, like a missing step on a dark stair. But it held. The wolf's jaws closed on air. He snarled, and backed away down the hall, and went out of the west door with nothing, after the sun, where he belonged.

Muninn climbed up her arm and hopped across onto the one-eyed man's shoulder. It remembered a frozen field, out loud, and then it was quiet, and it was home.

The one-eyed man took off his wide hat. Elsie knew him then. She had known him, really, since the gate of the field. Odin.

"Nothing worth having is free," he said, and he held out his hand.

She knew what he meant. She lifted the thong over her head and put the stone in his palm. It looked very small there.

"A raven lent for a sun found," said Odin. "That is fair. Some things are lent, not found."

She was on the riverbank in York at dusk, on the same Monday, with nothing round her neck, and Grandad was coming down the steps two at a time, calling her name.

There was nothing to show the coroner. Grandad laughed about it for a week. He laughed in the car and in the kitchen, and once, in the middle of the night, she heard him laughing through the wall.

"Lent, not found," he said, whenever she caught his eye.

The next Saturday he took her detecting anyway.
