The pit under the floor was cold and smelled of earth and apples, a darkness that settled over them like a heavy cloak. Above, the boards creaked with the weight of the house, while the fighting outside grew loud and sudden. Horns sounded from the broken wall, and running feet shook the dust from the air. Elsie watched Ketil, who spoke low to keep his hands from trembling. He talked about his mother’s farm, the goats, and the waterfall that froze solid every winter. The words floated up through the floor, thin and strange against the noise of the attack.

Inside, a shadow passed over the gap in the boards, and a pair of boots stood very still for a long moment. Elsie held her breath while the silence stretched tight as a bowstring. Then the boots moved, and the footsteps faded into the churn of the street. When the roar of the battle dimmed slightly, she pushed up a loose board and looked out into the street. The morning light was pale, and the smoke from the fires hung low and thick, turning the sky a dull grey.

She saw the crowd of Northumbrians moving through the smoke, their faces hidden by hoods and shields. The ground was slick with mud, and the air tasted of ash and wet wool. Elsie did not move, and Ketil did not speak, while the smoke curled up around her and filled the pit.
