Elsie pressed her back against the cold dirt of the pit, her breath held tight in her chest. Above them, the floorboards creaked under the weight of passing feet. The air down here was thick and stale, smelling of damp earth and the sharp, rotting sweetness of fallen apples. Ketil sat opposite her, his knees drawn up to his chin, his face pale in the gloom. He spoke in a low murmur, trying to steady his shaking hands. He talked about his mother’s farm in the north, where the goats bleated at dawn and the waterfall froze into a glass wall every winter. His voice was a thin thread in the dark, keeping the fear at bay. Suddenly, the noise above stopped. A footstep tapped on the boards directly over their heads, and the floor sank slightly. For a long, silent moment, someone stood still, looking down into the hole. Elsie stopped breathing entirely. The boards groaned again as the person turned and walked away. The footsteps faded into the distance, replaced by the distant crash of wood and the smell of smoke drifting down the gap. When the danger seemed to have moved on, Elsie reached up and pushed a loose board aside. A square of grey light fell across the dust. She squinted up through the gap to see the street. Smoke hung low over the houses, thick and choking. The Northumbrian soldiers had come over the broken part of the old wall at first light, and now the street was a blur of motion and dust. She could see the tops of helmets and the glint of spear-points, but no faces. Elsie pulled her hand back from the gap, her fingers black with dirt.
