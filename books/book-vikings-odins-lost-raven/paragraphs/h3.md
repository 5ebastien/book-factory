The horns sounded at first light, before the sun had cleared the low roofs. They came over the broken part of the old wall, and then the noise was everywhere at once. Shouting mixed with the sharp ring of iron on wood, and smoke rose from a thatch roof where the fire had caught. The air turned thick and grey, hiding the shape of the houses beyond the narrow lane. Ketil stood very still on the packed dirt, his face pale under the morning chill. He did not look at the fighting, but his eyes kept moving, searching for a way out. The women and children were already running for the river, their sandals slapping against the ground. They did not stop, and they did not look back as they squeezed through the crowd. Ketil looked at Elsie, then down at his own hands which were shaking slightly. He said he was going to hide, and he asked her to come with him. His voice was small, barely louder than the wind moving through the eaves. Muninn lifted off Elsie’s shoulder, beating its wings hard against the thin air. The raven flew straight into the smoke, moving toward the loudest part of the street. It remembered a road that ran through a battle, long before these walls were built. Elsie watched it disappear into the grey, her hand still raised where the weight had been. The smell of burning wood filled her nose, hot and dry and overwhelming. Ketil took a step forward, then stopped, waiting for her to answer.
