The long spit of sand curled out into the grey sea, holding the group in a narrow line. Wind bit at their cheeks, and spray hung in the air like fine dust. Bard stood near the edge, staring north-east where the sky had turned the colour of a deep bruise. It was not just dark; it was swollen, and the light beneath it looked sick.

Ketil pulled his coat tighter, his eyes fixed on the water as it churned against the hull of the Sea-Hart. He said nothing, though his hands worked at the rope until the knuckles turned white. Elsie watched the sky through the stone hanging round her neck, the glass warm despite the freezing air. Inside that circle, the dark mass of cloud did not look like weather.

Where the sun should have been hidden, a bright point had opened into something else. It was a light like a doorway, steady and sharp against the grey. Beyond that pale square, green branches moved slowly, as if a forest was breathing in the middle of the storm. The wind died down for a second, and the only sound was the low, heavy sigh of the waves.

Elsie looked at the sand at her feet, where the grains were shifting in a small, tight spiral. The stone in her hand felt heavy, and the cold crept through the padding of her coat. She watched the green branches sway once, then settle, while the bruise in the sky pulsed with a slow, dark rhythm.
