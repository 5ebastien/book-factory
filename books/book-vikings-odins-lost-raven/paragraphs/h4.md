The wind came off the North Sea in sharp, cold bursts. It sprayed salt against Elsie’s face, stinging her cheeks. The sand spit was narrow and pale, curling out into the grey water like a dead finger. To the north-east, the sky had turned the colour of a fresh bruise. Purple and swollen clouds pressed low against the horizon. The air smelled of wet stone and incoming rain.

Bard stood at the steering oar, his eyes fixed on the darkening sky. He wiped his hands on his tunic and looked at Elsie. "We can go now," he said. His voice was flat, stripped of warmth by the chill. "Or we pull her up on that sand and wait, and lose three days." He jerked his chin at Elsie. "What does the girl with the raven think?"

Elsie did not answer. She kept her trainers planted firmly on the wet deck. The wind tugged at her padded coat, trying to rip it from her shoulders. Around her neck, the sunstone rested against her skin. It felt cold, harder than the surrounding air. She pulled the stone closer to her eye, peering through its translucent surface.

In the real world, the clouds were a solid wall of grey. Through the stone, they were different. The darkness parted in the middle, where the sun should have been. A bright point of light opened wide, like a doorway carved into the air. Beyond that light, green branches swayed gently. They were impossible, vibrant, and far too far away.

Muninn landed on the wooden rail nearby. The raven shifted its weight, a low, creaking sound. He did not look at the sky. He looked at the water, his black eyes dull and reflective. The wind howled louder, tearing at the straw of Elsie’s hair. Bard stared at the bruised sky, waiting for the sea to make his choice for him.
