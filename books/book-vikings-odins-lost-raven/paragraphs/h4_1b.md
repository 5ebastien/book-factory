They hauled the Sea-Hart onto the shingle and rigged the sail into a shivering tent. The storm arrived at dusk, with a hiss of spray and a groan of canvas. It blew for two days, flattening the marram grass and coating every crust of bread in grit. Inside, the crew huddled over a wooden board, moving small bone pegs while their voices rose in angry debate. They argued over the rules as if the laws of the game were older than the sea itself. No one agreed on a turn. On the second night, Muninn woke Elsie by tugging her hair. She froze. Outside, far down the black spit of sand, a low howl cut through the wind. It was long and hungry. Elsie sat up, her trainers digging into the cold floor. Ketil shivered beside her, his eyes wide. "The wind," he whispered, though his voice shook. He missed his fjord farm already, and this sound made the dark feel infinite. Grim stood by the tiller, unmoving, the silver hammer on his chest catching the faint light. He watched the pegs on the board, his face unreadable. He did not look at the door. "We go now," Grim said. His voice was flat, like the swell outside. "The wind has dropped. We go in the dark, before it changes its mind." Bard wrapped his arms around his chest, eyes fixed on the shuddering sail. "We wait for the light," he replied, his tone sharp. "And we put in somewhere on the way for fresh water." The two men stared at each other. The tension in the small space was tight as a drawn bow. Elsie looked at the dry sand outside, then at the dark water beyond. She did not know which voice to trust. The wind pressed against the canvas, making it drum.
