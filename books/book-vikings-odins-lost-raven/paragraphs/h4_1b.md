The Sea-Hart lay beached on the long sand spit, her sail a white tent against the gray sky. The storm had arrived at dusk, blowing for two days until the grass was flat and the food was sandy. Cold crept into Elsie’s bones, as it always did in the North Sea, but this cold felt different, older. Inside the sail, the crew huddled around a wooden board, arguing over small pegs and rules no one remembered learning. Ketil moved a peg, frowned, and moved it back, his voice low with the weight of a homesick boy. Grim sat apart, his hand resting on the silver hammer he wore, watching the swell that was no longer there. He steered by the feel of the ship, and without water, he just watched the sand.

On the second night, Muninn pulled at Elsie’s hair, sharp and urgent. She sat up while the wind screamed past the canvas, a sound like a living thing denied its voice. Outside, far off along the beach, something howled. It was not a dog, and it was not the wind, though it carried the same hunger. Elsie pressed her ear to the cold fabric of the tent, listening to the silence that held the howl inside it. The sound faded, leaving only the scratch of sand against the sail and the steady, quiet breath of the raven beside her.

Ketil whispered that they should stay, that the storm would take them if they moved. Bard shook his head, saying the tide would not wait, nor would the water. Grim said nothing, only touched the silver hammer, as if weighing the weight of a choice he had made long ago. Elsie looked at the raven, then at the open gap in the tent where the dark beach waited.
