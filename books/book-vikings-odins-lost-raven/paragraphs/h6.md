The early spring light was long and pale, stretching low across the snow-capped peaks. Ice still clung to the shadows in the fjord, cracking softly as the crowd moved up the hill. Elsie stood near the edge of the trench, her trainers sinking into the cold, wet earth. The lady’s ship lay in the pit, a timber chamber waiting on its deck. Spades bit into the soil, turning over clods of frozen ground. They built the mound spadeful by spadeful, burying the wood under turf and stone. The air smelled of damp moss and woodsmoke from the fires nearby. Muninn flapped up to the top of the mound and sat in the smoke of the fires, looking back at her. Ketil stood beside her, his hands buried deep in his sleeves. He looked tired, the kind of tired that sleep could not fix. Elsie touched the sunstone at her throat. It was cold, like a pebble from the river. She closed her eyes and looked through it, just for a moment. The grey water of the fjord vanished. In its place was deeper water, moving slowly, and on it the lady's ship, afloat again, sliding away from the mound into the grey. Far beyond it, a tree stood alone on the horizon. It was not a tree from any forest she knew. It was waiting, branchless and still, in the endless light. She knew this was where the journey ended. She opened her eyes and lowered the stone to her chest. The mound rose higher around the ship, smoke curling from the fires on the slope. She turned to Ketil, her breath sharp in the thin air. She told him what she had seen, her voice low. He did not argue. He did not ask how she knew. He stepped forward and wrapped his arms around her. The hug was tight, pressing the air from her lungs. He smelled of wet wool and cold fire. "Go," he said, his voice muffled against her shoulder. "Do not look back. It is bad luck." Elsie pulled away, her heart beating a strange, heavy rhythm. She looked once more at the trench, then turned her face away.
