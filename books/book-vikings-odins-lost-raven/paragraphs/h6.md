The snow on the hilltops had melted into dark patches, and the light was pale and long.
The whole fjord came up the slope in a silent line to close the grave.
Spades turned the earth over in heavy clods, and the ship went under turf and stone.
Muninn sat on the rim of the trench, watching the workers with one eye.
Elsie stood at the edge of the crowd, her hand resting on the sunstone.
It was warm against her neck, humming with a quiet, low vibration.
She looked through the stone once more, past the shovels and the boots.
The grey water of the sea appeared, deep and still and endless.
Far beyond it, a single tree stood on the horizon, dark and thin.
The ship was sailing toward it, moving slowly on the glassy surface.
She knew, with a certainty that settled in her bones, that this was where she got off.
Elsie turned to Ketil, who stood beside her with his hands in his pockets.
She told him about the water, and about the tree waiting on the other side.
He did not argue, and he did not ask her how she knew such things.
Instead, he pulled her into a hug so hard it hurt her ribs.
He told her to go, and to not look back, because looking back was bad luck.
The mound rose higher around them, burying the last of the timber deck.
