The river was called Thund, and it roared wide and cold before the hall. Muninn remembered people who had tried to wade it. They were swept away. Elsie did not want to be remembered like that. Stepping stones sat in the current, green with weed and a long way apart. The water churned around them with a steady, heavy force. She took her trainers off. The cold bit into her bare soles immediately. She tied the laces together and hung them round her neck. The trainers swung against her padded coat. She stepped onto the first stone. It was slick and frigid. She held her breath as the current rushed past. She moved to the next one. The river roared louder here, close and loud. She planted her foot carefully. The stone did not move. She took another step. The weed clung to her leg. She shook it off, the motion making the water splash. Her hands trembled slightly in the air. The distance seemed endless across the grey surface. Muninn watched from the bank. The memory of swept-away people stayed with her. She focused on the next patch of green. Her toes gripped the wet rock. She leaned forward into the wind. The cold water swallowed her foot up to the shin. She held on tight. The world narrowed to the next stone. She pushed off. Her toes slipped on the wet moss, and she caught herself. Then she jumped from the last stone and landed on the far bank on her hands and knees, soaked to the shins, with her trainers still swinging round her neck. Behind her the river roared on as if she had never crossed it.
