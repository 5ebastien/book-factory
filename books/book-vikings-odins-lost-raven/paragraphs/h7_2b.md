Elsie stood at the water’s edge, her boots dry and heavy in her hands. The river Thund roared nearby, a wide and cold thing that did not invite anyone near. Muninn sat on a low branch, his black feathers still, remembering the cold. He spoke of legs slipping under ice, of water rising quickly to cover the nose. Elsie watched the current spin around the stones, each one green with wet weed. They were far apart, a long jump across the grey and white water.

She untied her laces and pulled her trainers off, letting the cold air touch her bare feet. She tied the shoes together and held them tight against her coat while the wind pushed at her face. The first stone was small, slick, and just a step away from the bank. She placed one foot on it, feeling the vibration of the river far below. Her toes curled around the rock, seeking grip that the ice refused to give. The water was so loud that she could not hear her own breathing, only the endless rush.

Elsie moved slowly, one stone after the other, until the last gap yawned before her. The far bank was solid, earthy, and safe, but her feet were shivering badly now. She hopped the final distance, landing on the mud with a soft thud that barely mattered. The river continued its roar behind her, a sound that seemed to grow larger even as she turned away. Her wet feet were pressed into the cold ground, and the mud gave way slightly beneath her weight. She stood there, trembling, while the water kept its heavy, constant song.
