The old Roman gate stood wide, filled with the noise and heat of bodies. Ketil’s voice was low and careful, cutting through the chaos as he pointed toward the throng. The Great Army had taken Jorvik on the first day of November, he said, and the air still smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool. Men in dark leather tunics moved in a slow, heavy stream, their iron-clad heads bobbing like dark stones in a river. None of them wore horns on their helmets, and the heavy wool caps they did wear were dark with sweat and dust.

Ketil pointed out the leaders from a long distance, where the shadows pooled thickest. There was Ivar, and Ubba, and Halfdan, standing apart on the gatehouse steps, though their faces were only blurs in the grey morning light. They were not people anyone would go near, and the men around them kept their hands open and their eyes down. Elsie shifted her weight, feeling the coarse feathers of Muninn beneath her padded coat, close against her shoulder. The raven was silent, but the air around them felt thick and waiting, like a breath held too long.

A man at the gate stood with a spear resting on his shoulder, his eyes moving over the crowd. He stopped, and his gaze landed on the black shape on Elsie’s shoulder. He did not speak, but he raised a hand and made a small, dry sign with two fingers. The sound of his breathing was the only thing Elsie could hear as the crowd parted slightly. The man stepped aside, his spear tip dragging lightly on the cobblestones.
