Ketil said the Great Army had taken Jorvik on the first of November. He spoke quietly, keeping his voice below the roar of the crowd. The old Roman gate was a mouth full of men. They were big and loud, shifting in the mud like heavy cattle. Not one of them wore horns on his helmet. Their gear was iron and leather, dull and practical.

Ketil pointed toward the center of the crush. Ivar stood there, arms folded. Ubba was beside him, shouting at a scribe. Halfdan leaned against a post, looking bored. They were not people anybody went near. The air around them felt thick with fear. Nobody crossed that invisible line.

Elsie kept her gaze fixed on the ground. Her trainers squelched in the wet dirt. She watched the flow of boots around her feet. The noise was a constant pressure against her ears. It was the sound of a dozen hundred men talking at once.

Muninn shifted on her shoulder. His claw dug into the fabric of her padded coat. He watched the gate with one black eye. A man at the gate looked up. He was tall, with a scar across his nose. He did not look at Elsie. He looked at the raven.

The man made a sign with his fingers. He snapped them together, once, sharply. It was a command. It was a permission. He stepped aside. The crowd parted slightly. Elsie felt the cold air hit her face. She stepped forward onto the uneven stones. Muninn remained still.
