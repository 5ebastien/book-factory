The door had a raven's wing nailed above it, dark and stiff against the wood. Inside, the air smelled of dried herbs and cold ash. Aud sat in the corner, her fingers wrapped around a heavy staff. She wore a string of glass beads that caught the dim light. She did not look at Elsie at first. Her eyes were fixed on the stone hanging around the girl's neck.

"A sun-stone," Aud said. Her voice was thin, like dry leaves rubbing together. She nodded slowly, knowing the weight of the thing before Elsie had a chance to speak. The raven shifted on its perch, its head turning to follow the old woman’s gaze. Aud bowed her head to the bird, a small, respectful dip. "Muninn's road goes north," she said, tracing the path with her finger in the air. It crossed the sea, where the ships went every spring. It went further, past the living world, to where only the dead could walk.

The market would have traders heading that way. The ships would carry them all northward. Elsie felt the stone warm against her chest, a faint pulse against her skin. The room seemed to shrink around them, the shadows pressing in close. Aud’s eyes were closed now, listening to a silence that Elsie could not hear. The raven was very still, its feathers smooth and black. Nothing moved in the room but the smoke rising from the hearth.
