The door had a raven’s wing nailed to it, stiff and black against the wood. Smell of smoke and dried herbs hung from the rafters. Elsie stood in the threshold, the stone warm against her chest. The woman inside was very old, her skin like crumpled paper. A string of glass beads clicked softly as she leaned on her staff. She looked at the stone before Elsie had even taken it out of her coat.

“A sun-stone,” Aud said. Her voice was dry as dust. “Clearer than any I have seen.”

Muninn stood on a beam above, his feathers ruffled. Aud bowed her head low, her back bent under the weight of years. She did not look at Elsie, but at the bird.

“His road home goes north,” she said. “Over the sea, to where the ships go every spring.” She paused, her eyes closing for a moment. “And past that. To where only the dead go.”

Elsie felt the cold of the house settle into her bones. The market outside was a distant rumble, a crowd moving through the streets of Jorvik.

“Traders will be going north,” Aud continued. “And the ships. They will carry him part of the way.”

She straightened up, though her knees seemed to creak with the effort. She held out a hand, not to take the stone, but to point. Her finger traced a line in the air, steady and unblinking. The herbs above them swayed, though there was no wind. Muninn hopped once on the beam, a black blur against the grey light. The stone pulsed once, a hot thump against Elsie’s ribs. Aud watched the raven, her face unreadable, waiting for the next memory to surface.
