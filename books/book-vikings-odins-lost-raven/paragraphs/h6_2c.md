High in the branches, the wind pushed against her coat, and the air smelled of wet wood and old ice. An eagle sat on a limb as wide as a lane, and its feathers were dark and still. A hawk perched between the eagle’s eyes, tiny and sharp, while four stags grazed on leaves that hung like green curtains. The stags did not look up, and the eagle blinked slowly, its head turning one inch at a time. Muninn ruffled his black feathers and looked at the bird, his beak clicking softly against the branch. He spoke only of what had happened before, remembering the wind and the weight of the air, and his voice was thin in the cold. Elsie leaned against the rough bark, her trainers gripping the branch, while the wind threw her hair across her face. Far below, the branches twisted into shadows, and far above, the leaves blurred into a gray sky. Then, over the tops of the branches, she saw it, a great hall that shimmered like a pool of light.

The roof shone like scales, bright and hard, and the wind did not touch it. Muninn looked at the hall, and his memory stretched back, remembering the sound of steel on steel and the roar of a crowd that never ended. Five hundred and forty doors stood in the side of the building, and each one was open to the cold. Shields hung on the roof, shining in the pale light, and spears formed the rafters, pointing like needles toward the sky. Inside, the brave dead fought all day and feasted all night, their voices rising in a low hum that Elsie felt in her chest. The eagle lifted its wings, and the wind gusted down through the branches, carrying the smell of smoke and iron. Muninn turned his head, watching the hall, while the stags below lowered their heads to eat. The branch shook gently beneath Elsie’s weight, and the leaf buds trembled in the sudden chill. She held her breath, waiting, as the first door of the hall began to swing open.
