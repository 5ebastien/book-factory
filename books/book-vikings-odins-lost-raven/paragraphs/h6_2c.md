The branches thinned out, but the wood was thicker than a car wheel. Elsie’s boots found purchase on the rough bark. The air smelled of pine needles and something older, like wet stone. Muninn flew beside her head, his feathers ruffled by the updraft. He said nothing, which was rare. Usually, the memories were constant. Here, the silence felt heavy. Then the leaves parted. The eagle was there. It was so large that its eye was the size of a dinner plate. It sat on a branch that looked like a fallen tree. Between its eyes, a small hawk perched, shrinking against the giant’s plumage. Four stags grazed on the leaves around them. Their antlers caught the dim light. They did not look up. The eagle moved one wing. A gust of wind hit Elsie in the face. It blew through the gaps in the branches, whistling. She shrank back, pressing her palm against the bark. Muninn remembered a voice. It was deep, and it said very little. The eagle spoke once. The sound was like stones grinding together. Elsie could not understand the words. But she felt the weight of them. Muninn remembered a squirrel carrying news down the trunk. The squirrel chattered, angry and fast. The news went to a dragon in the roots. The delivery was terrible. Elsie turned her head to look out over the branches. Far away, below the canopy, a hall sat on a plain. Its roof shone. It looked like scales, green and gold, reflecting a light that did not seem to come from the sun. Elsie squinted. The light was steady and cold. She stepped closer to the edge of the branch. The wind pulled at her coat. She held on tight.
