The chewing grew louder as Elsie moved lower. It was a wet, rhythmic sound, like someone gnawing a stale heel. She shifted her trainers on the slick, moss-covered bark. The mist here was thick and cold, clinging to her cheeks. She peered into the grey void below. A long, dark shape moved in the shadows. It was vast, a coil of black muscle and scales. The creature had its head buried in the Tree’s root. It chewed with a steady, unceasing motion. Sparks of rot flew from its jaws. This was Níðhöggr, the dragon of the deep. It had been at the root since the beginning of the world. It did not look up. It did not care that anyone was watching. The sound of its teeth grinding against the wood was deafening. Muninn ruffled his feathers, looking back toward the light above. The raven’s voice was flat and tired. He spoke of a memory that had no start. The Tree always grew back as fast as it was eaten. New bark sealed over the fresh wounds in seconds. The roots expanded to fill the gaps. This was the only reason anything was still standing. Without the constant repair, the world would have collapsed into the mist long ago. Elsie watched a slice of root break off and fall. It vanished into the darkness before it hit the ground. The dragon swallowed it whole. The chewing continued, loud and heavy.
