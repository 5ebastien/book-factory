The bells from the Minster rolled down the stone face, a deep and muffled thud. Elsie walked beneath the dark arches, the sunstone cold against her neck. She lifted one hand to her cheek, pressing her eye against the yellow crystal. The modern world did not move. It stopped.

The grey stone of the towers turned translucent, like frost melting on a window. Elsie’s breath fogged in the air, sharp and sudden. Under the thinning church, a lower shape rose up from the dust. It was made of rough-hewn timber and pale Roman blocks. A wall of old masonry ringed the whole thing, broken only by a single gate.

Muninn was perched on the gate’s crossbar. Its feathers were black and stiff in the wind. The raven did not look at Elsie. It looked at the wall, then at the ground, and its beak clicked open.

"I stood on this wood before," it said. The voice was dry, like leaves scraping stone. "When this place was Eoforwic. The name is older than the bells."

Elsie watched the raven. The Minster above them was becoming a ghost, its spires fading into the dark. The air smelled of wet earth and old smoke. She took a step forward. The ground beneath her boots felt different, less solid, like walking on packed snow.

The raven tilted its head. It remembered the weight of the wood beneath its claws. It remembered the noise of the river, louder than it was now. Elsie pulled her scarf tighter around her neck. The stone pulsed once, a warm beat against her skin.

She reached out a hand. Her fingers hovered over the raven’s wing, close enough to feel the heat of its body. The bell in the fading tower rang again, a distant chime.
