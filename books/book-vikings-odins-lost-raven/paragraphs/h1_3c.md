Elsie came out under the west front of the Minster as the bells started. She looked up at the towers through the stone, and they faded. The great church went thin, like breath on glass, and the cold air settled around her shoulders. Underneath it, something older and lower stood waiting. It was made of timber and rough stone, and a wall of Roman blocks ran round everything. The gate in that wall was dark, and the raven sat upon it. Muninn opened its beak, and a memory spoke out loud. It had been in this town before, when the town had another name. The name hung in the air, but Elsie could not hold it. The bells rang again, and the sound seemed to come from very far away. The Minster thinned further, and the old gate became more solid. The wood was wet with age, and the stones were cold to the touch. Elsie felt the sunstone burn against her neck. She stood still, while the world shifted its weight. The crowd on the street above did not see her. They walked on, through the ghost of the new city. Only the raven watched her, its eyes black and deep. The gate remained, patient and silent, in the fading light.
