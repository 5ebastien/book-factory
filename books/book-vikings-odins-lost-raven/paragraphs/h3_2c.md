The mud was thick and cold, sucking at her trainers as they moved along the bank. The water lapped at their knees, dark and silent in the grey light. Elsie could not feel her feet, a numbness spreading up her legs while the air bit her cheeks. Ketil pulled her down hard, dragging her behind a beached boat that listed heavily in the shallows. They sat in the silt, hidden by the hull, and watched the river upstream. A long boat cut through the current, the oars dipping in unison with a rhythmic splash. Northumbrian men rowed hard for the town, their faces set and tight with purpose. The sound of the oars was a dull beat, steady and close. Ketil’s hand was on her shoulder, his grip firm and trembling slightly. Elsie looked at the mud on his sleeve and the way he held his breath. The boat passed them, the water churning white around its prow. The men did not look back, their eyes fixed on the walls ahead. The river settled again, smooth and dark, reflecting the low sky. Elsie waited in the mud, the cold pressing in from all sides.
