The bank below the town was a mess of mud and boats pulled up anyhow. Elsie and Ketil waded along it, the water rising from their ankles to their knees in a cold that bit straight through their socks. Mud squelched between Elsie’s toes, thick and grey, sucking at her shoes with every step. Ketil kept his eyes on the path ahead, his face set in a grim line of concentration. The river Foss churned past them, dirty brown and fast, where it met the wider Ouse. Upstream, a long wooden boat cut through the current, its oars dipping in a frantic rhythm. Northumbrian men rowed hard, their breath visible in the chill air, aiming straight for the town. Ketil grabbed Elsie’s arm, his fingers numb and tight around her sleeve. He yanked her off the muddy path and down behind a beached, empty boat. They crouched in the shadow of the wood, huddled close to the cold hull. The water lapped at their shins, icy and relentless. Elsie stopped feeling her feet entirely. The cold had eaten the sensation away, leaving only a dull, heavy weight in her boots. Ketil pressed her gently but firmly against the side of the boat. He put a finger to his lips. His eyes were wide, fixed on the approaching shadow. Elsie could hear the splash of the oars getting louder, closer. The wood of the beached boat was slick and frozen under her hand. She looked up, where the raven sat perched on a low branch nearby. Muninn did not move. The raven’s black eye glinted in the gloom, reflecting nothing but the stillness of the wait. The Northumbrian boat rounded a bend, growing larger, the figures in it more distinct. Ketil held his breath. The water around their ankles churned against the stationary hull. Elsie looked at the muddy ground, at the frozen wood, at the dark water. She waited.
