The mud squelched between Elsie's trainers as she stepped onto the strand. A grey light hung low over the river, and the air smelled of tar, fish, and wood smoke. Dozen long ships lay drawn up on the bank, their wooden hulls scarred by salt and rope. Men moved with heavy purpose, rolling barrels up planks that creaked under the weight. Elsie lowered the stone from around her neck and felt its cold weight against her palm. The ships stayed, their shadows stretching long and dark across the wet ground. The raven dropped onto her shoulder, settling with a soft rustle of feathers. It had always sat there, or so it seemed, its eyes bright and black as the water. A man near the nearest ship looked up and saw her. He said the town was the army's now, and she had better keep out of its way. Elsie understood every word, though she had never heard him speak before. The crowd around him did not turn, nor did they ask who she was. They only kept working, their hands rough and brown from the labor. The morning was quiet, except for the sound of the river lapping against the hulls. Frost was melting into the mud, creating a cold that bit through her padded coat. Elsie stood still while the man turned back to his barrel, his shoulder hunched against the chill. The raven ruffled its feathers, a small, sharp movement against her coat. The town waited behind the ships, silent and grey.
