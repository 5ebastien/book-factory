The Shambles was dark between its leaning houses. Shop lights hummed against the glass. Tourists shuffled home, their voices softening. Elsie walked the cobbles, the sunstone heavy at her throat. Muninn perched on a sign above her head. He did not fly. He walked.

Every window she passed showed something odd. Through the stone, her reflection was gone. Instead, she saw a girl in a wool dress. The lane behind the girl was mud and grey. Then the vision shifted. She saw only the mud, deep and cold. Elsie let the stone drop against her coat. The cold glass pressed her collarbone.

The windows were windows again. They reflected her own face. Just a girl in a modern coat. The street noise rushed back in. The raven hopped down onto the cobbles. His claws clicked on the stone. He turned to look at her.

His eyes were black and still. They held no judgement, only memory. Elsie stood at the bottom of the street. The air smelled of rain and stale bread. She could leave him here. She could keep walking. The stone felt warm now, humming with a low vibration. It pulled at her neck, a quiet tugging sensation.

Muninn tilted his head. He waited. The shops were closing, their doors swinging shut. The tourists were gone. Only the sound of traffic from the square remained. Elsie reached for the stone. Her fingers brushed the smooth surface. The wolf was not here, but the cold felt close.

She did not move yet. The raven did not move either. They faced each other across the wet cobbles. The streetlights flickered once. Shadows lengthened on the old timber walls. Elsie’s breath made a small cloud. She held the stone tight. The decision hung in the air.
