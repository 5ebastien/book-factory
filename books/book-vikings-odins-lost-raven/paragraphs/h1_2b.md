The Shambles was narrow, with timber houses leaning together overhead until the street felt like a tunnel. Shop lights flickered on, and tourists hurried past with their coats zipped high. Elsie walked slowly, her hand resting on the sunstone that hung warm against her coat. The stone pulled gently at her neck, a steady weight in the fading light. Above her head, a raven walked along a shop sign, moving with a slow, deliberate pace. Its black feathers caught the orange glow of the lamps, and it did not flap or flutter. It simply stepped from plank to plank, watching her with one deep, dark eye. She stopped beneath a window, and the glass showed her something wrong. It was not her face; it was a girl in a wool dress, standing in a lane of thick mud. The girl looked out, and the mud looked cold and deep. Elsie blinked, and the window was just glass again, reflecting her own tired face. She let the stone drop against her chest, and the warmth faded into the cold air. The street was quiet, and the only sound was the distant hum of traffic on the main road. At the bottom of the lane, the raven hopped down onto the cobbles. It turned its head, and looked back at her. The stone in her hand felt heavier now, pulling downward with a slow, quiet insistence.
