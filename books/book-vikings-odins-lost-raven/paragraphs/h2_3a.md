The top of the wall was cold and wet under her fingers. Elsie gripped the stone while the wind pushed against her padded coat. To the west, the hills were dotted with fires, too many to count. They burned low and orange, like embers in a bed that would not go out.

Ketil stood beside her, his face white in the fading light. The Northumbrians had come back, people were saying. Both their kings were together, and they would arrive at first light. Men ran along the wall now, carrying shields and spears. Their boots scraped against the moss, a sharp, hurried sound.

Muninn ruffled his feathers on her shoulder. The raven remembered a wolf running through the snow. In his memory, the sun ran ahead of it, bright and fast. Elsie shivered, though the wind was not that cold. The fires on the hills did not move, only flickered.

The air smelled of smoke and wet earth. A single spear tip was visible near her knee, resting on the crenellation. The men kept running, their faces tight with worry. Elsie looked out at the distant lights, waiting for the dark to swallow them.
