Elsie stood on the town wall, her padded coat heavy against the chill. The evening air was sharp and tasted of smoke. To the west, the hills were dotted with fires, too many to count. They glowed orange against the darkening sky, like scattered embers. The Northumbrians had come back for their town, people were saying. Both their kings were there, ready to strike at first light. Men ran along the wall behind her, shields banging, spears bouncing. Ketil stood beside Elsie, his face white in the firelight. He stared at the hills, his mouth dry, his fingers cold. Elsie looked at him, then back at the fires. Something ruffled in her hair. Muninn shifted on her shoulder, a weight of feathers and old wood. Then the raven spoke, out loud, in its voice like a gate hinge, and what it said was a memory. A wolf ran through the snow, its breath misting the air. The sun ran ahead of it, a golden disc slipping over the horizon. The wolf chased, never catching, always close. The memory was cold and vivid, filling her mind until she shivered. Elsie pulled her sleeves down over her hands. The stone hung cold against her chest on its thong. Ketil turned, his eyes wide, waiting for a signal. Elsie watched the fires.
