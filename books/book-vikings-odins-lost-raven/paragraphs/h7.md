The black water of the lake did not move. It lay flat and still, a mirror for the dark canopy of the Tree above. On the island in the center, a shape rose from the reeds. Elsie squinted, her breath hitching in her throat. She had taken it for a small hill, a bump in the earth, until the top of it swelled with a slow, rhythmic rise and fall. A wolf. It was too large to be real, its fur matted and grey like wet ash. A ribbon bound around its wrists and ankles was so thin it looked like a strand of silk. It shimmered with a faint, impossible light. This was Gleipnir, woven from six things that had no physical form. The wolf’s head turned. One yellow eye, bright as a burning coal, fixed on her. The beast did not growl. It spoke, its voice a low rumble that vibrated in Elsie’s chest. Sköll was coming, it said. The sound of its words faded into the silence of the water. On a rock nearby, two ravens stood. Muninn was familiar, his feathers sleek and black. Beside him sat Huginn, quick and restless, his head twitching. The two birds did not look at each other. They stared at Elsie. Huginn opened his beak, his voice sharp and insistent. Why would she choose the path that Sköll expected? The question hung in the cold air. Muninn ruffled his feathers, looking away toward the far bank. He remembered an old road, a path that wound round the base of the hills. It was long and dark and full of thorns. The ribbon on the wolf’s leg pulsed with a faint, silver glow. At the far edge of the lake, where the water met the mist, a bridge appeared. It shimmered, thin and brittle, like a crack in the ice.
