The island sat in the black lake, and the wolf lay upon it. He was so large that Elsie mistook him for a hill. The fur was still and grey, until the creature breathed. Fenrir opened one yellow eye and looked at her, while the ribbon held him down. It was thin as silk, and it was made of six things that do not exist. A cat’s footfall and a woman’s beard were woven in, and so were a mountain’s roots. Bear’s sinews and fish’s breath ran through the thread, with a bird’s spit to finish. Gleipnir hummed softly as it pulled tight against the wolf’s thick paws. He spoke then, and his voice was slow and deep in the cold air. He said that Sköll was coming to take the stone from her hands. The wind died down, and the water went black and very still. Huginn landed on a rock beside Muninn, and the two ravens did not agree. Huginn asked why she would go the way that Sköll expected. Muninn remembered an old road that round the hills, long and dark. The bridge at the far edge shimmered in the cold light. It waited for her.
