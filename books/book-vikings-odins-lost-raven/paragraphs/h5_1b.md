The fjord shore smelled of wet pine and cold salt. Snow clung to the peaks, while ice patched the shadows below. Long, pale light stretched across the water. Asgaut knelt in the gravel, a small bald man with chips of stone in his beard. He held a chisel and a hammer, working the lady's stone. The flat rock faced the sea, where every passing ship could read the words. Ketil stood behind him, shivering and looking toward the boat. Elsie stepped closer to watch the carver's hands. Asgaut paused and pointed to the carved lines. "Her daughter raised this," he said, tracing the marks. "For her mother." He read the runes aloud, finger moving slowly. "Sixteen runes," he announced, tapping the final line. "All straight. Made for cutting." He looked up, noticing Elsie’s stillness. "That is all anybody needs to write anything." He turned the stone slightly, letting the light catch the grooves. Elsie studied the angles, her mind clicking into place. There was no curve for a proper E. So names were spelled as they sounded. She touched the rough edge of the stone. Muninn sat on a nearby rock, silent as a memory. The carver returned to his work, the hammer striking with a sharp, rhythmic crack. The vibration hummed up through the gravel into Elsie’s knees. She watched the chip fly away, a tiny fragment of history. The lady would be buried in her ship in three days. Everything she needed was packed inside. Asgaut struck again, the sound echoing off the frozen peaks. He did not stop. Elsie remained still, her hand resting on the cold, sharp stone. The hammer rose and fell, steady and relentless.
