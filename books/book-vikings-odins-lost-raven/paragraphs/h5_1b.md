The pale light hung low over the fjord, and the ice in the shadows crackled softly as the air warmed. Asgaut stood on the shore, a small bald man with chips of grey stone in his beard. He was cutting the lady’s stone, facing the sea where every passing ship would see it. Elsie watched his hands move slowly, the chisel tapping against the hard rock.

The stone said her daughter had raised it in memory of her mother. Asgaut read it to her, pointing with a dirty finger, rune by rune. There were sixteen runes, he said, and all were straight lines because they were made for cutting. That was all anybody needed to write anything.

He noticed her watching him, and he stopped working. The chisel rested in the groove he had made, still and cold. Elsie looked at the letters, feeling the strange weight of the words she could now read. The great lady of the fjord had died in the winter. In three days, she would be buried in her ship, with everything she needed for the journey.

Asgaut’s eyes were tired, but his voice was steady as he explained the marks. There was no proper E in the old shapes, so names were spelled the way they sounded. Elsie thought of her own name, Elsie, and how it would look carved into this grey rock. The wind moved across the water, bringing the smell of salt and wet stone.

He turned back to the stone, the chisel lifting again. The sound was sharp and clear, a single tap that rang out in the quiet air. Elsie stood still, her hands in the pockets of her padded coat. The ice near her trainers began to melt, forming small, dark pools in the dirt.
