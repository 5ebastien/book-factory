Five hundred and forty doors lined the front of the hall, each one wide enough for eight hundred warriors to march out side by side. Elsie stopped counting after the first few. Looking up at the sheer scale of it, she believed the old poem. The roof was a solid patch of overlapping shields, and the rafters were bound spears pointing inward like a nest. Over the west door, a carved wolf and eagle hung frozen in a timeless struggle, their stone jaws bared.

It was evening outside, and loud. The air was thick with the smell of roasted meat drifting out from the cracks and the heat of the fires inside. Warriors came pouring through the lower doors, shaking snow from their clothes and armor. The noise was a physical weight, a roar that vibrated in Elsie’s chest. She stood still, her trainers planted on the frozen ground. Muninn sat on her shoulder, his black feathers ruffled by the draft. The raven tilted his head, his eye catching the firelight.

Then he spoke. He did not use words. He pushed a memory directly into her mind. It was a sharp, cold sensation, like biting into a frozen apple. Elsie felt the ground beneath her feet settle. She felt the specific warmth of the firelight on her face. She felt the weight of the long day lifting off her shoulders. It was not a thought. It was a fact, clear and undeniable. It was home.

Muninn shifted, his claws gripping her padded coat. The memory lingered, bright and steady. Elsie looked down at the raven. The crowd surged around them, a river of noise and movement, but the feeling remained. She was exactly where she needed to be. The wolf and eagle above the door seemed to watch, their stone eyes still. The roar of the hall faded into the background, a dull hum. She stood still, letting the feeling settle in her bones.
