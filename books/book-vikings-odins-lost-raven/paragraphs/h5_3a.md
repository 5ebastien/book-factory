Asgaut winced at the first strike. The mallet felt heavy in Elsie’s hand. She swung it down, and the chisel chipped a shard of rock. It flew across the sand and buried itself in the grass. Asgaut made a noise like a stuck door. He did not help. He stood with his arms crossed, watching the chip come out. Elsie aimed for the next rune. Her hand trembled. The metal scraped against the stone, leaving a white scratch. It did not cut deep. She pushed harder. Her jeans were dusty at the knees. The padded coat was unbuttoned, hanging open in the breeze. Asgaut flinched again. He pointed a finger at the groove. Elsie ignored him. She was focusing on the shape. The afternoon dragged by. The shadow of a pine tree stretched long across the sand. The light did not seem to move. Her arms ached under the sleeves. She cut the final rune with a frustrated shove. The chisel slipped. The mark came out crooked. Asgaut leaned over to look. He wiped his hand on his tunic. He nodded slowly. "Best crooked name," he said. "I have ever seen." He reached out and took the mallet from her grip. He lifted the stone with both hands. Elsie watched him carry it to the edge of the shore. He set it up, facing the sea. The waves washed against the base. The ships would see it from the water. Muninn settled on the stone. The raven turned its head. It looked at the carved letters for a long time. Muninn spoke, remembering the shape of the marks. The memory was faint. Elsie reached up to her neck. The sunstone hung there, cold. The low sun came through the clear crystal. It hit the runes directly. The letters blurred.
