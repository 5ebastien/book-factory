The chisel felt cold in her hand, and the mallet was heavier than it looked. She struck the flat stone, and Asgaut winced again, though the blow had been soft. The first mark was shallow; the second, deeper. Elsie guided the tool with care, trying to keep the lines straight. The wind off the fjord pulled at her hood, whispering against her neck. It was a slow work, chipping away the grey surface to reveal the lighter rock beneath. Each strike sent tiny fragments flying into the sand. She did not look up for a long time, only counting her breaths. The stone was hard, and her fingers grew numb from the cold.

When the last rune was finally cut, she stepped back to look at it. It was crooked, the lines bending slightly to the left. Asgaut leaned in, his face close to the rock, and sniffed the air. He said it was the best crooked name he had ever seen, his voice flat and dry. He did not smile, but his eyes stayed on the stone for a moment. Then he straightened up and walked to the edge of the shore. He picked up the piece of stone and carried it to a patch of flat rock facing the sea. He set it upright, packing sand around its base to hold it firm. Now the ships passing by would see the name, carved into the landscape.

Muninn landed on a driftwood log nearby and tilted its head. The raven looked at the stone for a long time, remembering it, the way it remembered everything. There was no new memory in its eyes, only the old ones turning over like leaves in water. Elsie stood still while the low sun moved across the sky. The light came through the sunstone round her neck, which hung against her padded coat. It did something odd to the runes, making them look wet, though they were dry. The shadow of the carver’s shore fell long across the sand. She watched the sea line where the water met the stone, waiting for the light to change.
