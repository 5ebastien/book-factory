Through the stone, the image sharpened. A grey wolf, vast and low, ran flat out after the sun. It was not a vision of something far away. It was here. Much nearer than she had thought. The creature turned its great head, sniffing the air. It sought the small sun round her neck. Elsie felt the cold bite of that attention on her skin.

Ahead, the hall rose on the slope. Its roof was a tapestry of shields, overlapping like scales. Rafts of spears formed the rafters, pointing down into the darkness below. Inside, the brave dead fought all day and feasted all night. It was a place of endless noise and warmth, invisible from this frozen slope.

Muninn ruffled his feathers. He spoke in memories, not words. He remembered that the hall had five hundred and forty doors. No wolf could enter through any of them. The stone pulsed against her palm, warm as a heartbeat. Then the memory shifted, darker and less happy. Muninn had never seen a wolf try.

The wolf was still running. It was getting closer. The shadow it cast stretched long across the grass. Elsie stood on the slope, the stone heavy in her hand. The hall waited above, silent and solid. The wolf waited below, eager and hungry. She looked at the path up the hill. The air was thin and sharp. Her fingers tightened around the smooth, cold rock.
