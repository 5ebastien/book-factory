The cold bit through her padded coat before the light did.
Below the slope, the roof of Valhalla waited.
It was made of shields, thousands of them, layered like scales.
The rafters were spears, crossed and locked tight.
Inside, the brave dead would fight all day and feast all night.
Muninn spoke in a voice like dry leaves scraping stone.
It remembered the hall had five hundred and forty doors.
It remembered that no wolf could enter by any of them.
Then it remembered, less happily, that it had never seen a wolf try.
Through the stone, Elsie saw the grey shape again.
It was the size of a barn, running flat out.
Behind it, the sun ran ahead, wild and fast.
The wolf turned its great head every few strides.
It sniffed the air, looking for the other sun.
The small one round her neck pulsed against her skin.
He was nearer than she had thought.
Much nearer.
The wind died, leaving only the smell of iron and old blood.
The shield-roof gleamed, cold and sharp in the fading light.
Elsie stood still on the slope, the stone heavy in her hand.
The wolf’s head turned once more, locking onto her.
