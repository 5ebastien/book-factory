The rope bit into her palms as the wind pulled them out onto the open water.
The Ouse stretched wide and grey before them, winding through the flat wet country.
Bard stood at the helm with his eyes closed, and the smell of wet wool filled the air.
Ketil hauled on the line beside her, and his face looked tired but less broken.
Behind the boat, the smoke from the town thinned and drifted away like old wool.
The sun dropped lower, and the light turned the water into a sheet of hammered tin.
Elsie held her breath and watched the smoke vanish into the growing dusk.
Somewhere far off, the riverbank disappeared into the mist and the dark trees.
The boat leaned over, and the water hissed against the hull in a long, slow rush.
She thought of the fires on the hills, and the silence that had followed them.
Ketil said he was going home, and the words sounded small in the cold air.
Muninn sat on the rail and said nothing, as if remembering was enough.
The wind dropped, and the sail went limp with a heavy, soft thud.
The boat stopped moving, and the water beneath them went still and deep.
