Elsie wrapped both hands round the rope. It was thick and rough against her skin. The Sea-Hart was already moving in the water. Below the town, the wind came from behind them. Bard stood at the tiller, his hands steady on the steering oar. He looked out at the flat, wet country stretching ahead. He signaled to Ketil and Elsie to haul the main sheet. They pulled together, straining against the weight. The great square of wool caught the air with a sharp crack. It smelled like a wet sheep. Red and white stripes snapped tight. The ship leaned hard to the starboard. Elsie felt the deck tilt under her trainers. She gripped the rope harder. The Sea-Hart ran fast. Behind them, the smoke of Jorvik got smaller and smaller. It rose in thin grey lines into the sky. Ketil laughed for the first time since the fires on the hills. His face was bright and free. "I am going home," he said. He looked at Elsie, his eyes shining. Muninn sat on the gunwale with his feathers puffed up. Then he spoke, in his gate-hinge voice, and it was a memory: a storm on this same sea long ago, a black wall of cloud coming down from the north-east, and a ship that did not put in to the sand in time. The wind filled her padded coat. The river Ouse wound through the reeds. The water was brown and cold. They were far from the shipyard now. The old shipwright was gone. The ship moved on. The smoke disappeared. The wind kept blowing.
