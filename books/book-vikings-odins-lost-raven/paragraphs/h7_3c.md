The gate stood alone on the hillside. It was very old and locked. No wall ran to either side of it. Muninn remembered the name Valgrind. He remembered that few knew how its lock was shut. The raven said nothing else. He just looked at the iron bars.

Elsie walked around the gate. There was no wall to stop her. She felt silly going in a circle around a locked door. The grass was short and dry under her trainers. On the far side, the hall was there. It had not been a moment before. Now it filled the view.

Its roof was made of shields. Row upon row of them, overlapping like fish scales. Some were dented. Some were dull with age. They caught the faint light under the Tree. The rafters were spears. Hundreds of them, lashed together with rope. They formed the bones of the roof. The wood was dark and heavy.

There were five hundred and forty doors. Elsie did not count them. She could not. There were too many. They were all closed. Inside, she knew, the brave dead fought all day. They feasted all night. They drank mead from horns. They sang songs of battle.

The air smelled of smoke and old wood. It smelled of iron too. A cold draft pushed against her cheeks. Muninn ruffled his feathers. He looked back at the gate. Then he looked at the doors.

Elsie stood still. Her breath was visible in the air. The cold was biting at her fingers. She looked at the nearest door. It was tall and dark. A handle of bronze hung from it. The hall waited. The shields gleamed. The spears stood ready.
