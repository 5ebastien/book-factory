Inside, the hall was so long that its far end was blue with smoke. The air smelled of smoke and roasted meat, and the cold from the walls seeped through Elsie’s padded coat. She stood still while the noise rushed over her in great waves, loud and bright and endless. Hundreds of warriors sat on the benches, their armor shining dull red under the firelight. They shouted for mead and meat, and the sound of their voices shook the floor beneath her trainers. Nobody looked at her, and nobody looked at the raven on her shoulder.

Muninn tilted his head, his feathers dark against the gray wool of Elsie’s coat. The one-eyed man sat on the high seat at the far end, hidden in the smoke. Two wolves lay at his feet, still as stones, while the man’s single eye stared out into the dark. Elsie knew him, and she had last seen him on a bench in Museum Gardens. Her hand moved to the sandwich bag in her pocket, where the stone sat cold against her thigh. The mead flowed, and the laughter rose, and the smoke swirled around her head. She waited, her breath shallow, while the hall seemed to breathe in and out.

The warriors drank, and the cups were filled again. The smoke thickened, and the far end of the hall faded into a blue mist. Elsie’s finger touched the stone, and the metal was cold, then warm. She looked up at the high seat, and the one-eyed man did not move. The wolves did not move. The hall was quiet now, or it seemed so, as the laughter died down into a hum. Elsie held her breath, and the raven let out a soft, low cry. The sound hung in the air, thin and sharp, until the smoke swallowed it whole.
