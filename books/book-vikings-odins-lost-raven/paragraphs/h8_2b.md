The air inside the hall was thick and heavy. Smoke hung in the rafters, turning the far end of the room blue. It did not smell like the damp field where they had found the stone. It smelled of roast meat, hot mead, and sweat.

Bench after bench stretched away from Elsie’s feet. Warriors sat in long rows on both sides, their shoulders pressed together. They were huge and loud, shouting over the crackle of the fires. Shields hung along the wooden walls, painted with faded red and white shapes. No one looked at her. A girl with a raven on her shoulder was nothing. It was just a shadow moving through the noise.

Muninn ruffled his feathers. He leaned in, close to her ear, and whispered a memory. He spoke of a bench in Museum Gardens, the stone cold in a plastic bag, and the abbey ruins standing in the grey morning light. Elsie closed her eyes. She remembered exactly where she had been. She knew this place in her bones before she saw it.

At the very end of the hall, a seat rose high above the rest of the room. A man sat there. He wore a wide hat that hid his hair. One eye looked out from beneath the brim, dark and still. Two wolves lay at his feet, their heads resting on paws. They did not move. They did not breathe, exactly, but they were there. Elsie’s breath caught in her throat. She had seen him before. Not here. Not in this firelight. She had seen him on a bench, waiting, while she held the stone. The hall went on and on, but her gaze stayed fixed on that high seat. The wolves’ ears twitched once, and the man in the wide hat did not look away.
