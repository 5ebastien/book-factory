The Sea-Hart was long and low. Oak planks overlapped like fish scales. Ketil’s uncle, Bard, sat in the stern. He was broad and weather-wise. He wanted to see the stone. Elsie pulled it from around her neck. The cold air made her breath mist. Bard held the stone up to the sky. He looked for a bright point. He found it. Then he whistled. It was a short, sharp sound. He lowered the stone slightly. He looked at Elsie with sharp eyes. "Some say stones like this find the sun in fog," he said. "But I have never met a man who owns one." His voice was dry and flat. Elsie watched his face. He did not seem angry. He seemed interested. He turned the stone over in his hand. "What do you want for it?" he asked. Elsie shook her head slowly. "It is not mine to sell," she said. Her voice was quiet. Bard looked at the raven, Muninn, perched on the gunwale. The bird said nothing. It only watched. Bard nodded once. He put the stone back in her hand. The wood of the ship creaked under his weight. He leaned back against the mast. The morning light was still cold. The smell of tar hung in the air. Elsie tucked the stone away. Bard looked out at the water. He was thinking about the fog ahead.
