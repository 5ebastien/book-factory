The air in the back yards was cold and still, smelling faintly of wet straw and old mud. Behind every house, a fence of woven hazel stood guard, and inside them, pigs rooted through the dirt. Elsie and Ketil moved through the spaces between the buildings, stepping over the low fences and round the soft bodies of the animals. It was nearly quiet back here, a strange silence that felt heavier than the noise from the street.

In one small yard, an old woman bent over a line of white cloth. She was calmly taking her washing in, her hands moving with a slow, steady rhythm. To Elsie, the woman looked as if the fighting on the street were nothing more than rain against a window. The old woman did not look up as they passed, her eyes fixed on the damp linen. She only spoke when she had tied the last square to the wooden peg. She told them the Sea-Hart was still resting on the shipyard slip. She said Bard and his crew had gone down to the staithe. She had not seen them come back since the morning light broke.

Ketil stood very still, listening to the wind move through the branches of a bare tree. The wood creaked once, a dry sound in the frozen air. Elsie reached out and touched the hazel fence, the woven sticks hard and rough under her palm.
