Behind the houses, every yard was fenced with woven hazel.

Elsie and Ketil moved through them one after another.

They went over low fences and round fat pigs.

The noise of the fighting was still out front, but here it was nearly quiet.

A smell of damp wool and old wood hung in the air.

Muninn flew low, his black feathers tight against his body.

In the center of the nearest yard, an old woman stood by a line of clothes.

She was calmly taking in her washing, as if the noise were just rain.

She did not look up when Elsie approached the gap in the fence.

"Is the ship still there?" Elsie asked.

The woman paused, holding a wet tunic in her hands.

"The Sea-Hart is on the slip," she said.

Her voice was steady and dry.

"And Bard?" Ketil asked from behind her.

"Bard and his crew went to the staithe," the woman replied.

"They did not come back."

Elsie looked at the washing line.

The fabric was heavy with water and dripping onto the frost-hardened grass.

The old woman handed the tunic to a child who was standing nearby.

The child disappeared into the house with the wet cloth.

The woman turned back to the line, her face unreadable.

Ketil shifted his weight, his eyes fixed on the closed door of the yard.

The wind picked up, rustling the dry leaves in the rubbish pit.

Elsie felt the cold seep through her trousers.

The old woman’s hands moved slowly, picking up another shirt.

The fabric was rough and grey.

She snapped it once to shake out the wrinkles.

The sound was sharp in the quiet yard.

Elsie watched her fingers work.
