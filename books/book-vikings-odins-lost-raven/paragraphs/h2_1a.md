The market mud sucked at Elsie’s trainers, thick and cold against her soles. Noise rose around her, a wall of shouting and haggling that made the air feel heavy. Walrus tusks gleamed white on a stall, next to furs that smelled of wet dog and old smoke. Silver coins with curly writing were cut into pieces, weighed on small folding scales by men who did not look up. Nobody counted them; the bits were just metal, mixed and sold by weight.

Elsie felt the sunstone warm against her neck, a dull heat that did not match the cold air. Muninn sat on her shoulder, his feathers black and still, watching the crowd with one side of his head. He did not move, but his weight was real, pressing down on her padded coat. She tried to blend in, to be just another person holding a bird, but the market did not feel right. The people moved around her in a slow, thick wave, eyes sliding over her and sticking.

A boy near the root beer stall was arguing, his voice high and sharp over the din. He wore a tunic stained with tar, and his hair was tied back with a strip of leather. He was losing the argument, his face turning red as the merchant shook his head. This was Ketil, a ship's boy on the Sea-Hart, which would sail north when the army allowed it. He had not seen his mother’s fjord in two winters, and the memory of that cold shore was a fact he carried like a stone.

Ketil stopped arguing and turned to Elsie, his eyes landing on the raven on her shoulder. He did not ask if she was okay; he asked why a raven was sitting there. Muninn ruffled his wings, the black feathers shifting into a shape that remembered the open sea, the ships going home. The boy’s hand went to his belt, then stopped, hovering in the air as he watched the bird’s eye blink slowly.
