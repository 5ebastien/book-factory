Jorvik’s market was a pit of mud and noise. The air smelled of wet wool and roasting meat. Stalls crammed together, shouting over one another. Walrus tusks from the far north leaned against buckets of yellow amber. Furs piled high, thick enough to hide a child. Silver coins with curly writing sat in shallow dishes. These had come all the way from the Caliphate. Nobody counted the coins. They cut them into bits and weighed them on little folding scales. Elsie walked through the crowd, the sunstone cold against her chest. Muninn sat on her shoulder, silent and heavy. A boy stood near the furs, arguing with a merchant. He was about her age, wearing a tunic stained black with tar. His face was red, and his breath came in short bursts. He was losing the argument over a pair of bone skates. The merchant snapped, "Too big for you, boy," and turned his back. The boy turned, and his eyes locked on the raven. He stopped walking. He pointed a grubby finger at Muninn. "Why is that bird on your shoulder?" Elsie said nothing. The boy stepped closer, his boots squelching in the mud. "I’m Ketil," he said, his voice dropping. "Ship’s boy on the Sea-Hart. We sail north when the army lets us. I’ve not seen my mother’s fjord for two winters." He looked back at the skates, then at the raven. "My mother says a raven that rides on you is luck," he said. "Up north, over the sea. Where the ships go every spring." He held out his hand, still holding the strap of the skates.
