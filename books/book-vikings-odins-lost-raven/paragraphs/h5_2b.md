The air in the trench was cold and sharp, smelling of wet earth and old wood. The lady lay in the small timber house on the ship’s deck, dressed in her fine clothes. Her things were laid around her in neat rows, waiting for the mound to cover them all. A tall woman with a ring of keys at her belt stood over the body. She was dry-eyed and in charge, moving with a quiet, heavy purpose that made the crowd hold its breath. Everyone on the hill stood very still, as if the wind had stopped to listen. Elsie looked through the stone in her pocket, her fingers cold against the rough surface. The image shifted, the wooden planks dissolving into grey, rolling water. The ship was not in a trench at all, but sailing forward, its sail full and white against the dark sea. It was moving towards something that looked like a tree, tall and thin in the distance. Muninn spoke, his voice low and rough, like leaves scraping on stone. This was a road, he said, and the memory hung in the air, heavy and still. Elsie watched the grey water move, and the sound of the ship’s wake grew loud in her ears.
