Asgaut’s beard was dusted with grey chips. Elsie asked him about the dream first, the one with the tree in it. He did not laugh. "Everybody dreams of the Tree before a burial," he said. "It means the dead one is already on her way." Then he pointed a nail at the flat stone by the water. "Sixteen marks," he said. "Straight lines. Enough for anything." He did not have an E. Elsie’s name would be spelled as it sounded. She watched the nail scratch the surface. The sound was sharp and dry. Asgaut watched her watch. He looked annoyed. She learned the shapes quickly. Faster than he expected. Her fingers itched to trace the air. He moved on to the next rune. The water lapped at the wet sand. Muninn sat on a rock, silent as a stone. Asgaut scanned the shore. He picked up a small grey rock. It was the size of a loaf. "For the sea," he said. "Every crossing leaves a name." He held it out to Elsie. The stone was cold and rough. "I can cut it," Asgaut offered. "Or you can." Elsie took it from his hand. It weighed almost nothing. Asgaut shook his head. "You will be crooked," he said. "Everyone is." He pointed to the others on the shore. They were further back, faceless. He was not looking at them. He was looking at her hands. The stone felt heavy in her palm. Asgaut waited. Elsie looked at the rune on the stone. It was just a line. She held the rock out between them, and did not decide yet whose hand would cut it.
