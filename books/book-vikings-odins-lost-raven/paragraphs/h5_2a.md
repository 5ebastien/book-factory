The fjord shore smelled of wet sand and old salt. Asgaut knelt by a flat grey stone, his beard heavy with small white chips. He held a nail point sharp against the surface, scratching lines that did not curve. There were only sixteen shapes in the old way, and they had to do for everything. Elsie watched his hand work, the stone dust puffing in the thin air. It felt easy, the way he made the angles meet. She thought the shapes waited for the nail to tell them where to go. When he finished, he looked up with a small, annoyed frown. She had learned faster than he had expected, and he did not like being outpaced. He did not say this, only tapped the nail against his thumb. "There is no proper E," he said quietly, his voice low against the wind. Her name would have to sound like something else, spelled out in straight cuts. He stood up and brushed the dust from his knees, the grey sand sticking to his coat. Then he picked up a smaller stone, about the size of a loaf, smooth and cold. He set it before her on the ground, near the edge of the cutting area. "You leave your name behind," he said, "before you cross the sea." It was a rule for everyone who went, a mark left for the ships passing by. He could cut it for her, or she could do it herself. If she did it, the lines would be crooked, and the letters might stand too tall. Elsie reached out, her fingers hovering over the rough surface of the small stone.
