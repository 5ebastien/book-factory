Elsie sat at the end of a bench with Muninn on her shoulder, and she ate the boar. It was better than it had any right to be, warm and rich against the cold air. The warriors on either side of her told stories of their deaths, and they said the words as if they were funny. Mostly, they were. She chewed slowly while the laughter rose around her, a low hum that vibrated in her chest.

Inside, the hall was so long that the far end was blue with smoke. Long fires burned in the center, and benches of warriors ran down both sides. Shields hung along the walls, and the light from the fires made the metal look like oil. The man on the high seat stood up, and the room went quiet. Bench by bench, the silence moved all the way down the hall. He looked straight at her.

Muninn ruffled his feathers, and a memory slipped into Elsie’s mind. She saw the boar sleeping in the embers, whole and unbroken. In the morning, it would stand up again, fresh and clean. It was boiled every night, and it was whole again every day. She looked down at her hands, which were sticky with juice. The man on the high seat was still watching her.

The noise did not start immediately. It came back in pieces, a spoon clinking, a deep cough, then the murmur of many voices. Elsie kept her eyes on the meat, which was slowly running out. She did not look up again, and she did not ask why the dead were so loud.
