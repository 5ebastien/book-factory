Elsie sat at the end of the bench, her legs swinging without touching the floor. Muninn perched on her shoulder, his feathers slicked back against the heat of the long fires. In her hands was a piece of boar, pulled from a mountain of bones that stretched toward the back of the hall. The meat was tender and rich, better than it had any right to be. She chewed slowly, watching the warriors on either side. They were loud and cheerful, banging their tanks against the table. A man with a scarred cheek told a story about falling off a horse. He laughed until his ribs ached, and the men around him roared in agreement. It was mostly funny. Elsie bit into the boar again, the grease running down her wrist. Muninn turned his head, his black eye catching the firelight. He did not speak. He only watched the smoke curling up into the blue distance. Halfway through the meal, the noise began to change. The laughter died away, bench by bench, like a candle being pinched out. Silence spread from the center of the hall toward the ends. The fighters stopped eating. They turned their heads to look. At the far end, on the high seat, a man stood up. He was tall and thin, with a beard that hung in grey ropes. He did not shout. He did not raise a weapon. He simply looked down the length of the hall. His gaze moved past the hundred men, past the hanging shields, and stopped. He looked straight at Elsie. The hall held its breath. The fire crackled. Elsie held her chew.
