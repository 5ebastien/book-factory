The fjord shore was cold, and the air smelled of salt and wet stone. Asgaut held a small grey rock in his left hand, steady as a heart. In his right, a small knife caught the light, quick and neat as a bird in flight. He cut the first mark into the stone while Elsie watched, and chips flew like tiny white sparks. The sound was soft, a dry scrape against the rock, then another, and another.

Elsie asked him to cut something more underneath her name. She wanted it to say she had raised the stone in memory of her grandad. Asgaut looked at her with flat, quiet eyes, and he did not ask why. He knew her grandad was not dead, yet he lifted the knife again. He cut the words carefully, his hands moving without a single shake.

When it was done, he set the stone up on the sand, facing the sea. The words faced the water, so passing ships could read them. Elsie stood there, and she had to look at the water for a while. The wind pushed the surface in long, slow lines that never broke.

Muninn sat on a rock behind her, black and still, watching the carver. The raven’s eye was bright in the dull light, a small point of memory. Elsie looked at the stone, then at the water, and back at the stone. The grey rock held the name, straight and true, waiting for the tide.
