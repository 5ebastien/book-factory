The small grey stone sat in Asgaut’s palm. He watched Elsie watch him. The chisel struck.
Chips flew, white sparks in the cold air. He cut quick and neat.
Runes appeared, one by one. Straight lines made for cutting. There were sixteen of them.
That was enough to write anything. There was no proper E in the set.
So he spelled her name the way it sounded. Elsie checked the letters as they formed.
It looked strange, but it was right. She asked him to add more.
He paused, the tool hovering. He looked at her, then at the stone.
She told him to cut it underneath. She had raised the stone in memory of her grandad, Len.
Asgaut frowned. He did not understand. Her grandad was not dead.
It made no sense to him. But he did it anyway.
The second line took longer. The chips were finer.
He worked with a steady rhythm, a soft tapping sound.
When he finished, he set the stone up on the shore.
It faced the sea, ready for passing ships to read.
Muninn stood on a rock nearby, feathers ruffled by the wind.
The raven said nothing. It only looked at the new mark in the rock.
Elsie stood still, her hands hanging at her sides.
The water lapped at the sand, cold and relentless.
She had to look at the sea for a while.
The waves broke, foam dissolving back into the dark.
The stone waited, solid and grey against the horizon.
