The riders came over low. They ran on the air above the open plain, and the grass rippled beneath them without wind. The light off their mail was like light off water, bright and cold. They were bringing the brave dead to the hall. One of the women looked down at Elsie as she passed. It was a long look. Elsie looked back. The woman’s face was calm. Then they were gone. The grey light was ordinary again. It did not shimmer or change. Muninn stood on a low stone near Elsie’s feet. The raven tilted his head. He spoke in memories. He remembered a ship breaking waves. He remembered the smell of wet wood. He remembered a song sung in a deep voice. Elsie listened to the sounds in her mind. Then the howl came. It was nearer than before. The sound of the wolf chasing the sun was close. Elsie felt a chill in her bones. She stood still on the plain. The grass was cold against her trainers. Muninn looked at the horizon. The riders were far away now. The howl faded, then started again. Elsie’s hands were at her sides.
