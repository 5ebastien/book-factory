The grass on the open plain rippled without wind, and the grey sky hung low above the roots. Elsie stood still under the tree, her trainers sinking into the damp earth. A howl rose from the distance, thin and long, and it stopped as suddenly as it began. Then the air moved, not with wind but with weight, and the grass flattened in wide, silent paths. Women on horses came over the low ground, running on the air itself. Their mail caught the weak light, shimmering like the surface of a dark lake. They carried the brave dead to the hall, a procession without sound. Elsie watched the horseman closest to her, a woman with a pale face and eyes like flint. The rider looked down, holding her gaze for a moment that stretched too long. Elsie did not look away; she felt the cold in her chest match the chill of the stone in her bag. The procession passed, a blur of leather and iron, and then they were gone. The plain was still again, the grass rising slowly to its previous height. Muninn ruffled his feathers, a low rumble in his throat, but he said nothing. The howl came back, closer now, and the sound scraped against the silence. Elsie’s hand tightened on the sandwich bag, the stone pressing hard against her palm.
