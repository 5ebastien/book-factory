Huginn’s way went straight across an open plain of grass that rippled although there was no wind. He flew ahead of her, his questions landing in her ears like small stones. Was she tired? Was she cold? Had she noticed the light getting dimmer?

She had. The grey sky pressed low over the plain, and the grass moved in slow, white waves. Behind them, where the great Tree stood, a shadow was coming across the ground faster than any cloud should move. It stretched long and thin, swallowing the green as it ran. Huginn asked whether she could run any faster.

She could, it turned out. Her trainers gripped the loose dirt, and her legs moved with a rhythm she did not know she had. The wind was not in the air; the wind was in her chest. She looked back, and the shadow was close now, a dark stain on the bright grass. It did not have a shape, yet she knew it was watching.

Muninn sat on her shoulder, silent for once. The raven remembered the cold, he said, and the long run through the snow. Elsie smelled the wet earth and the sharp scent of ozone in the dry air. Her breath came out in short, white puffs that vanished before they hit the grass. The shadow stopped at the edge of the ripples, and the plain went still around it.

Elsie’s hand touched the rough bark of a nearby tree; it was dry and warm to the touch.
