The grass stretched out in every direction, a flat sheet of green that quivered without a breeze.
Huginn flew ahead, his wings beating in a steady, sharp rhythm.
He did not wait for an answer to his first question.
Was she tired?
Elsie shook her head, though her legs felt heavy and leaden.
The raven kept moving, his black feathers stark against the grey air.
Was she cold?
She pulled her padded coat tighter around her shoulders.
The wind did not blow, but the chill was a physical weight on her skin.
Huginn circled above, asking if she had noticed the light changing.
She had.
The world was dimming, a slow bleed of color from the edges of her vision.
Behind them, the shadow of the Tree moved across the plain.
It was not a shadow cast by a cloud.
It traveled faster than anything that could fall from the sky.
The grass rippled again, a shiver running through the stems.
Huginn asked if she could run any faster.
Elsie felt the tension in her calves, the gears of her body engaging.
She could.
The raven flew on, leaving her with the question hanging in the thin air.
The shadow grew larger, swallowing the light near the horizon.
