The well was round and wide and full of water so white it looked like milk. A root of the Tree came down into it from above like the leg of a table, and beside it three women sat on stools, spinning. They were the Norns. Muninn said so, very quietly, and then got inside Elsie's coat and stayed there.

One of them was very old, older than anybody Elsie had ever seen, with hands like roots. One was about Elsie's mum's age, with her sleeves rolled up; one was younger than Elsie, ten perhaps, with bare feet and a sharp little face. Every so often the old one put down her spindle, dipped a bucket in the well and poured the white water over the root. It was so the Tree would not die, she said, without being asked. It had been dying since the beginning, and they had been watering it since the beginning.

The old one looked at Elsie. It was not an unkind look, but it went all the way through, and Elsie had the feeling that she knew about the hamster and the lie about the homework and everything else, all the way back. The youngest looked at her and smiled. Elsie did not like the smile at all.

The middle one was the only one who was busy. Her spindle turned and turned, while the grey thread ran off it and down into a basket, on and on.

"That's what's happening now," she said, nodding at the thread. "All of it."

She pulled out an arm's length, bit it off with her teeth, and tied it round Elsie's wrist in a knot that would never come undone.

"Anyone who sees that," she said, "will know who sent you."

Somewhere behind Elsie the grey light flickered, as if something had passed in front of it. The youngest Norn's smile grew wider.
