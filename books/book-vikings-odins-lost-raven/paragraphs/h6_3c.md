The pool was so still that at first Elsie thought it was a hole in the world, with nothing under it. Then she knelt at the edge and saw that it was water, and that it went down further than any water should. Muninn remembered its name out loud: Mímir's well, where whoever drank knew things. Far down at the bottom, very small, something pale lay on the stones and looked up. Elsie looked at it for a long time before she understood that it was an eye; Muninn remembered that too. Its master had given that eye for one drink from this well, and had thought it cheap.

There was a black bird on the far rim. It was a raven, the same size as Muninn and nothing like Muninn at all, because he could not keep still. He hopped and turned his head and hopped again, and when he spoke his voice went up at the end of everything.

"Who are you?" he said. "Why are you here? Do you know what that is, round your neck?"

Muninn came out of Elsie's coat and stared at him. It remembered a name, very softly. Huginn.

"Is that him?" said Huginn. "Is it really? Where has he been?" He hopped closer along the rim. "Do you know how far you still have to go? Do you think you can do it on your own?"

Elsie opened her mouth to answer, but Huginn did not wait. He twisted round, pulled a long black feather out of his own wing with his beak, and dropped it into her hand, where it shone blue-green in the grey light.

"Wouldn't a stranger help a girl carrying one of Odin's feathers?" he said. "Wouldn't they?"

Then he cocked his head. Far away, under everything, there was a sound like a dog growling in its sleep.

"Can you hear that?" said Huginn.
