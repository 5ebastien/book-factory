Elsie stopped at the bottom of the steps. The high seat waited at the far end of the hall, raised on stone. Two wolves lay at its foot, still as statues. Sköll stood a dozen paces behind her. The wolf was grey and enormous, and the air around him tasted of iron. Elsie looked up. The man in the wide hat looked down. He did not pretend he was not there. He was the man from the bench in Museum Gardens. He was the man at the gate of the frozen field. His one eye was bright and dry. He held out his hand. He waited for the raven. Muninn ruffled his feathers on Elsie’s shoulder. The bird said nothing, only remembering the weight of the sky. The man spoke without moving his lips much. “I paid an eye for what I wanted,” he said. “Nothing worth having is free.” He looked past Elsie to the wolf. “Sköll will have the stone,” he said. “Unless somebody stops him.” Elsie’s hand tightened around the stone in her fist. It was cold against her palm. She thought about the bench. She thought about the frost on the field. She thought about the way some things are lent, not found. The man’s hand was still. He waited. Sköll shifted his weight. The floorboards creaked under the wolf’s paws. The raven’s beak clicked once. The silence in the hall was heavy and thick. Elsie stood in the middle of it. The man’s outstretched hand hung in the air.
