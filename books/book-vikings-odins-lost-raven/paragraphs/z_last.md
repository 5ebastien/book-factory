Elsie stopped at the foot of the high seat, where the two wolves lay still in the fading light. The one-eyed man looked down at her from the raised wood, and he did not pretend he was a stranger. He was the man from the bench in Museum Gardens, and he was the man at the gate of the frozen field. Sköll stood a dozen paces behind her, grey and enormous, and the whole hall held its breath in silence.

The air was cold and smelled of old ash, while the fires at the hall's edge bent away from the wolf's shadow. The man held out his hand, his fingers pale and waiting for the raven to land. He said he had paid an eye once for what he wanted, and that nothing worth having was free.

"Some things are lent, not found," Elsie said, though the words felt thin in the quiet air.

The man nodded slowly, and his remaining eye was dark and deep. He said the wolf would have the stone unless somebody stopped him, and he looked at Elsie as if he already knew the answer. The raven shifted on her shoulder, its feathers bristling against the cold wind that had no source. Elsie looked at the open hand, then at the grey shape behind her, and the silence stretched out like a wire.
