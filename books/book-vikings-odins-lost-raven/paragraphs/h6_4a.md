The grey light beneath the branches grew dimmer, fading as if a cloud had crossed a sun Elsie could not see. Through the stone, a bright point jerked forward, moving like a rabbit running through tall grass. Behind it, something vast and far off ran after the light on long, cold legs. Sköll was closer now, and he had turned his head to sniff for the stone in her pocket. Muninn slid inside her coat without being asked, settling against her spine with a heavy, warm weight. A second raven dropped from the high branches, landing on a low twig with a sharp crack of wood. Huginn shook his black feathers and looked at her with bright, restless eyes. He asked if she intended to stand there all day, watching the world go dark. The leaves around them shivered in a wind that touched nothing else. Elsie felt the stone grow warm against her chest, pulsing like a slow heartbeat. She looked at the shadow moving through the grey undergrowth, too far away to be truly seen. Huginn asked again, his voice sounding like dry leaves skittering over stone. Muninn said nothing, only pressed closer into the fabric of her coat. The air smelled of wet earth and old smoke, thick and still. She held her breath, listening to the distant snap of a twig under a heavy paw.
