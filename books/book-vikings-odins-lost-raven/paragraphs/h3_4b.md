The wind had dropped by late afternoon, and the Ouse moved slowly through the flat wet country. Elsie sat in the bow with Muninn, looking ahead while the crew rowed. She saw the herons standing in the shallows, and a drowned tree that broke the surface with a black snag. Further on, a village appeared on the bank, and its people stood in the grey light to watch the ship go by. They did not wave; they simply stood, silent and still.

Where the river bent, the tide was coming in against them. Bard had them drop anchor and wait for it to turn. The oars rested in the racks, and the water lapped quietly against the hull. Muninn remembered the sea out loud, a grey road with no end to it, and a storm on it. The memory hung in the air, cold and heavy, while Elsie wished it had not spoken at all. The anchor chain creaked softly as the current pulled, then stopped.

The sun sank lower, turning the water to a dull, metallic silver. Ketil sat nearby, pulling his collar up against the growing chill. Elsie watched the village lights begin to flicker on one by one, small and far away. The river was very quiet now, save for the soft tick of the water against the wood. Muninn’s eyes were closed, and the storm in his mind felt closer than the sea. The anchor held fast in the mud.
