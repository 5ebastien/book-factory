Elsie sat in the bow of the dragonship, the cold wind biting at her exposed cheek. Beside her, Muninn ruffled his black feathers and watched the water slide past the wooden hull. The crew rowed in unison, the oars dipping with a heavy, rhythmic splash that marked the seconds. Elsie looked ahead, scanning the flat, wet country that stretched out on either side of the winding river. A line of herons lifted from the reeds, their white wings stark against the grey sky. A few moments later, they passed a drowned tree, its skeletal branches reaching up from the murky water like a warning. The current slowed as the afternoon deepened, and the boat began to drift. In the distance, a small village appeared on the bank, its stone houses huddled together against the chill. People stood on the shore, their faces turned toward the passing ship, watching silently. Elsie felt their eyes on her, a heavy weight that settled in her chest. She looked at the horizon, where the river began to widen, the current fighting against the incoming tide. The water rose against the bow, and the boat rocked gently on the swell. Bard called out from the stern, his voice cutting through the wind and the creak of the ropes. He ordered the anchor dropped, and the chain clanked down into the dark water below. The ship came to a halt, hanging in the middle of the river, waiting for the tide to turn. Muninn opened his beak and spoke, his voice like dry leaves skittering across the deck. He spoke of a grey road with no end, a storm rolling over a vast, empty expanse. The memory hung in the air, cold and distant, and Elie wished he had not said it. She stared at the water, the anchor chain taut and silent beneath the surface, holding them fast in the slowing current.
