The light broke grey and thin, and the smoke was already thick in the lanes. Elsie kept her sleeve over her mouth and followed the black shape that cut through the haze. Ketil stayed close behind her, his face pale and tight with cold. She could not see the fighting, only the edges of it drifting past.

A wall of shields blocked the end of the street; it looked like a single, moving body. A horse stood in the middle of the road, with no rider on its back. Near the fence, a man sat holding his arm, and he did not look up at them. The air smelled of wet ash and old blood, heavy and warm against her skin.

Muninn did not stop for any of it. It flew low and steady, turning corners as if it had done this a thousand times before. It had seen this before, in other towns and other times, and it did not seem afraid. Elsie ran with her eyes fixed on its wings, and the ground felt soft under her trainers.

The raven landed on a broken gatepost and turned its head to look at her. It remembered another battle, in another town, with the same grey light and the same smell of smoke. Ketil stopped walking, and the street went very quiet for a second. The raven blinked, and the memory hung in the air between them.
