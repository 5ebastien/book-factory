The light came up gray and thin, but the fighting was already everywhere. Smoke hung low in the streets, thick and tasting of ash. Elsie pulled her sleeve up over her nose and mouth. The air was cold and wet against her bare hands. Muninn flew low, its black wings barely clearing the mud. It did not panic. It moved with the calm of a thing that had seen this before. Elsie followed, her trainers slipping on the slick cobbles. Ketil came after her, his face pale and tight. She never saw the fighting properly, only the edge of it. A wall of shields blocked the end of the lane, a shimmering curtain of wood and iron. A horse stood in the middle of the road, its head down, no rider. Near the fence, a man sat slumped, holding his arm. He did not move when they passed. The noise was a constant roar, deep in her chest. Muninn led them round the shield-wall, then round the horse. It did not look back. It did not look at her. It simply flew ahead, into the thickening smoke. Elsie matched its pace, one foot in front of the other. Ketil’s breathing was quick and shallow beside her. The world was just noise and gray dust. Then Muninn stopped. It landed on a wooden gatepost, its claws gripping the wet wood. It turned its head, one eye dark and bright. And it remembered. The memory was calm, a picture of another town, another battle, long gone. The smoke swirled around the post, cold and still.
