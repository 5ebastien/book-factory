The bark of the Tree was like a cliff face, rough and grey, and it tasted of old air. Elsie held her fingers to the ridges while the cold bit through her coat. Above her, a small red blur scrambled down the trunk with startling speed. It stopped on a low branch, tail twitching, and it looked directly at her trainers.

"You are walking like a dead boot," it said.

The voice was high and sharp, and it made no effort to be polite. Elsie opened her mouth, but the squirrel was already moving again. It ran up, then down, carrying words like stones in its cheeks. It spoke to the sky, where a shadow waited, and to the dark roots below, where something breathed.

"I can carry that insult for you," the squirrel said, freezing mid-stride. "To anyone you like."

"No," Elsie said. She had nothing rude to say.

The squirrel looked disgusted by her answer. It turned its head slowly, as if smelling a bad smell.

"The wells are further round," it said.

It pointed one small claw toward the mist. The mist did not move, but it seemed to listen. Muninn, perched on a higher branch, remained silent. The raven watched the squirrel, and the squirrel watched the mist. Elsie’s hand was still on the bark, and the wood was warm under her palm. She did not let go.
