The squirrel dropped from a branch, landing on the trunk with a thump. It was red and small, with a tail like a brush. This was Ratatoskr. It looked at Elsie. It did not blink.

"Those shoes are an insult to the earth," it said. Its voice was high and sharp. "Do you know that?"

Elsie blinked. She looked at her trainers. They were muddy and old. "They are comfortable," she said.

Ratatoskr shuddered, a full-body shake that rippled from nose to tail. "Comfort is a lie told by the tired. I have seen the eagle’s feathers. I have smelled the dragon’s fire. And you wear dead leaves on your feet."

Muninn shifted on his perch nearby. He said nothing. He only watched, his black eye dull and deep. The raven spoke in memories, not words, and this moment was too new for him to hold.

"I can carry a message for you," Ratatoskr continued, scurrying up the bark. "To anyone you like. The eagle. The dragon. Even the roots. I will tell them exactly how ugly your footwear is."

Elsie frowned. She did not want the dragon to know about her trainers. "I do not have an insult for you," she said.

The squirrel stopped. It turned slowly. Its eyes were narrow and green. "That is worse. That means you are boring. And rude." It looked disgusted. "The wells are further round. Follow the smell of wet stone. Do not get lost in your own feet."

It pointed a tiny claw down the trunk, then scrambled away, disappearing into the upper branches. The bark was rough under Elsie’s hands. It felt like cold stone. Above, a shadow passed, vast and winged. Below, a low hum vibrated through the wood.

Muninn tilted his head. A memory flickered in his eye: a well, dark and deep, water rising to the brim. But it was old, centuries old. It did not help with the present.

Elsie looked at the path the squirrel had indicated. The mist was thick there. She looked at the raven. Muninn did not move. He did not agree or disagree. He simply existed in the quiet.

Elsie’s fingers were stinging from the climb. She let go of the bark. The mist drifted past her face, cold and damp. She did not move. She waited.
