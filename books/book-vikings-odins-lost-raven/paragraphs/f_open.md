The top step was cold under her trainers. Elsie stood still on the high seat, while the hall held its breath and the fires burned low and blue. Muninn shifted on her left fist, his feathers flat against the wind that blew from nowhere. In her right hand, the sunstone glowed, a small, hard light that cut through the dust of the evening. The floorboards groaned once, a deep, heavy sound that traveled up through the wood and into her bones.

Sköll came up the hall for the stone.

He was grey and enormous, his paws silent on the ancient oak. The wolf’s eyes were fixed on the light in her hand, not on her face. Elsie did not run. She turned to face him, her coat thick with dust, and felt the stone pulse against her palm. Behind her, the one-eyed man in the wide hat stood at her back, his hat brim casting a long shadow over his single eye.

The light from the stone touched the wolf’s face, and the grey fur turned to white frost. Sköll stopped, his nose twitching once, while the silence in the hall grew louder and thicker. Muninn spoke in a memory of rain, a small, clear sound that hung in the air between them.

"Do you remember the sun?" Elsie said, her voice steady and plain.

The wolf did not answer. It tilted its head, and the firelight bent away from its shoulder, leaving a cold space where the warmth should have been.
