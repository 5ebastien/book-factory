Sköll was already a dozen paces behind her in the hall. The wolf’s grey fur looked like wet ash, and his eyes were bright points of gold. The fires along the walls bent away from him, their flames turning blue and dying. No one moved. The warriors held their breath, their helmets clinking quietly in the sudden stillness. Elsie did not run. She turned to face the wolf, her knees locking straight. The one-eyed man in the wide hat stood behind her, a solid wall of wool and silence. He said nothing. He did not need to. Elsie lifted the stone higher. The light from it spilled over the wolf’s snout, casting long, sharp shadows across the floor. Sköll stopped ten feet away. He lowered his head, his nose twitching as he sniffed the air. The sound of his breathing was loud in the quiet hall, a wet, heavy rasp. Muninn leaned forward on Elsie’s fist. He spoke a single memory, a sound of wind and ice that echoed in the air. Sköll flinched. The wolf’s ears flattened against his skull. Elsie held her ground, her jaw tight. The stone grew colder, biting into her palm. Sköll took one step forward, then stopped. He watched the light, and Elsie watched his eyes. The outcome was clear, though no one spoke it.
