Above her, the rafters were spears laid side by side, and they held the smoke of a hundred fires. Elsie stood in the shadows, holding the wooden gap, while the noise of the hall rushed up to meet her. It was a sound like wind through a deep cave, but warm. Below, the benches were crowded with warriors who ate and drank in groups. Horns of mead were passed from hand to hand, and the liquid glowed amber in the firelight.

The air smelled of roast meat and old wood, and it was thick enough to taste. She watched a goat walk between the legs of a seated man, but nobody looked at it. The hall stretched out long and wide, ending in darkness at the far wall. There, on a high seat of carved bone, sat a man in a wide hat. He had one eye, and it did not blink. Two wolves lay at his feet, curled together like puppies.

Muninn went very still on her shoulder. Its feathers lifted slightly, and it made a sound that was not a croak. It remembered him, it said in her head, and the memory was heavy as stone. It remembered him so hard that it could not say anything else. The man on the high seat did not eat, although the food sat cold before him. He only watched the fire, and his hand rested on the sword hilt. Elsie held her breath while the seconds passed, and the sound of the hall seemed to grow further away.

The raven’s beak clicked softly once. It was a dry sound, like a bone breaking in the snow.
