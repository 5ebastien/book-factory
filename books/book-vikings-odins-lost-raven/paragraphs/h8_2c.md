Up among the rafters, the air was thin and cold. Elsie pulled herself between the crossed spears. They were old and dry, like dead branches. Below, the hall stretched out in a long, dark line. Fires burned low in the pits along the floor. The light was orange and still. Hundreds of warriors sat on benches that ran the length of the room. They were still. They did not move. Horns of goat’s mead passed hand to hand. The liquid was thick and golden. It sloshed gently as the hands turned. At the far end, the hall narrowed. A high seat sat alone in the shadows. A man in a wide hat sat there. He had one eye. Two wolves lay at his feet. Their fur was the color of winter. They did not sleep. They watched the fire. The man did not eat. He did not drink. He sat very still. Muninn went very still on her shoulder. Its feathers flattened against its body. It said nothing at first. Then it spoke, low and rough. It remembered him. It remembered the snow falling on the seat. It remembered the cold biting its legs. It remembered the silence after the last horn was empty. It remembered the weight of the one eye looking through it. It remembered him so hard that it could not say anything else. The raven’s beak opened and closed. No sound came out. Only the memory pressed against Elsie’s arm. The fire crackled once. The mead horn stopped moving. Elsie held on to the spear. The wood was rough under her gloves.
