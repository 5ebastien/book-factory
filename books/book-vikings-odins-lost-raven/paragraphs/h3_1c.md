The lane shook with their feet. Elsie ran with the crowd, past women holding babies and old men leaning on sticks. Children carried hens over their heads, squawking into the thin air. Ketil ran beside her, his arms full. A small girl sat in his arms, silent and grey. She had lost her mother, though nobody said so.

Then a woman shouted a name. It cut through the noise of the running crowd. The woman reached out and took the girl from Ketil without a word. Ketil’s hands stayed open for a second before he let go. He did not look back. Elsie did not either. They kept running until the lanes met the sand.

The smell hit them first, salt and tar. The strand at Jorvik stretched out before them, grey and flat. Ships were already pushing off, full of people and heavy with goods. The water lapped at the hulls, quiet and black. Muninn found them there, dropping onto Elsie’s shoulder with a soft thud. The raven looked out at the water, then back at her.

The Sea-Hart was not one of the moving ships. It sat still on its rollers, far up the bank at the shipyard. Nobody was with it. The ropes were slack. The wind tugged at the rigging, a small, dry sound. Elsie’s fingers tightened on the raven’s feathers. The ship was empty and waiting.
