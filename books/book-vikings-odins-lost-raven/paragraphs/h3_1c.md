The lane shook with the weight of hundreds of feet. Elsie ran, her breath tasting of ash and salt air. Beside her, Ketil held a small girl tight against his chest. The child was quiet, her eyes wide and black like beetles. A woman in a torn grey cloak stepped out of the way. She shouted a name, sharp and broken, and reached for the girl. Ketil let go without a word. The crowd surged forward, pushing Elsie down the slope toward the water. The ground turned from packed mud to loose shingle under her boots. It crunched loudly in the silence of the fleeing people. The river was grey and choppy, littered with broken wood. Muninn landed on Elsie’s shoulder with a heavy thud. His claws dug into the wool of her sleeve. He looked past her, his head tilting as if listening to a distant drum. Ahead, the first ships were pushing off into the current. They were full, heaving with people and sacks. The Sea-Hart was not among them. Further along the strand, the shipyard stood empty. The great vessel sat on its rollers, stripped and bare. No crew was visible on the planks. Only the wind moved the flapping rope at the mast. Elsie stopped, her hands shaking at her sides.
