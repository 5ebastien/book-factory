The fog lifted all at once, like a sheet pulled off a table. There was Norway. Grey rock went straight up out of the sea, and snow lay heavy on the tops. A narrow gap cut through the stone; it was a fjord. Ketil stood in the bow, his hands loose at his sides. He did not say anything for a long time. The ship rocked gently, and the ice in the shadows groaned low and soft.

Then he began to speak. He named every farm on the ledges, his voice thin against the wind. He named the waterfalls that fell white and silent from the high cliffs. Elsie watched his face, and she saw that his eyes were wet. She looked down at her padded coat and pretended not to notice. The raven sat on the gunwale, its feathers flat and dark. It said nothing, but it seemed to remember the light.

The pale morning light stretched across the water, long and cold. It touched the snow on the peaks, which glowed faintly in the distance. The smell of salt and old stone hung in the air, sharp and clean. Elsie’s breath misted in front of her, then vanished into the chill. She held the small shield tight, the wood cold under her palms.

Ketil’s voice trembled on the name of his mother’s farm. The water below was dark and still, reflecting the grey stone above. The ship moved forward, slow and steady, into the mouth of the fjord. The cliffs closed in on either side, tall and waiting.
