They hauled themselves up the rope ladder, trainers slipping on wet wood. The Sea-Hart groaned as they weighted the deck. They rowed out past the rocks, and the sail went up, and for a long time there was only the fog and the creak of the oars. Ketil moved to the bow, his hands white-knuckled on the railing. Elsie stood beside him. The wind bit through her padded coat. Muninn landed on a stanchion, ruffling his black feathers. He looked out at the mist. The water was pale and still beneath them. Ice drifted in the shadows of the hull. The light was long and thin, early spring. Then the fog lifted. It went all at once. Like a sheet pulled off a table. There was Norway. Grey rock went straight up out of the sea. Snow sat on top of the cliffs. A gap in the rock opened wide. It was a fjord. The sea inside was dark and deep. Ketil did not say anything for a long time. The ship cut through the waves. He was looking at the rock face. His eyes were wet. Elsie saw him wipe his cheek. She looked at the water instead. Then he spoke. His voice was steady. He named every farm. He named every waterfall. They went in between the cliffs. The air smelled of salt and cold stone. The water fell from the cliffs above. It sprayed the deck. Ketil kept looking. He kept speaking. The name of the next farm hung in the cold air. Elsie braced herself against the mast. The wood creaked under her hand. The fjord narrowed.
