Up on his shoulders, she could see everything. His arms were like oak beams, strong and steady. The wind tugged at her hair, but she held on tight. High above the yard, the world looked different. The long hall stretched out below, vast and grey. Smoke curled from its shield-covered roof. Benches filled the interior, crowded with warriors. They laughed and shouted, slamming cups together. The noise carried up to her, a dull roar. She squinted against the cold air. Something moved on the roof ridge. It was a goat, white and shaggy. Its head dipped into a green leaf. The tree had grown right through the hall. The goat chewed, calm and sure. Its sides swelled with golden liquid. Mead flowed from its teats. It was enough for every man inside. Elsie watched the stream run clear. Muninn ruffled his feathers beside her. He looked tired, his eyes bright. He did not speak. He only remembered. The memory was sharp and sudden. A feast, a song, a scent of honey. Elsie turned her head slowly. She scanned the far end of the hall. Shadows pooled in the corners. A high seat rose above the rest. A man sat there, still. He wore a wide hat, black and brimmed. His face was pale, his skin tight. One eye stared out at the hall. The other was covered, a scarred lid. He had not looked at her yet. He sat in silence, tall and straight. The mead continued to drip, drop by drop. Elsie's breath misted in the cold. She waited, perched on the warrior's shoulder. The goat chewed its endless leaf. The hall hummed with life below.
