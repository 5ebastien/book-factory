The warrior’s hand was warm on her shoulder, and the ground was far below. From up high, Elsie could see the entire yard, where the long benches were filling up with tired fighters. A tree grew through the middle of the great hall, its branches heavy with green leaves. On the roof, a goat stood grazing among the twigs, its head turning slowly as it chewed.

She watched the animal for a moment, and then saw the stream. Instead of milk, a golden liquid ran from the goat’s side and dripped down into the eaves. The smell of honey and spices drifted down to them, thick and sweet in the cold air. Elsie blinked, but the sight did not change; the roof was wet with it, and the drop fell with a soft plink.

Down in the hall, the noise rose like a tide. Laughers and voices mixed together, and the fires crackled against the darkness. At the far end, a high seat waited, and in it sat a man wearing a wide hat. He had one eye, and it was closed. The red-bearded warrior said nothing, but he shifted his weight slightly.

Elsie looked at the roof again, where the goat kept eating. The mead ran steady and calm, a clear line in the shadow. She looked back at the hall, at the moving sea of the dead. The one-eyed man had not looked at her yet, and his other eye was hidden by his hand. The cold air bit at her cheeks, and the raven stayed silent.
