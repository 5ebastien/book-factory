The wind died away as the grey sky lightened. Elsie rubbed the salt from her eyes and waited for the sun. It rose clear and cold, a white disc that cut through the thin mist on the water. She had not seen such a sky since the frozen field near York. Grim pointed ahead, and his hand stayed there. Far on the edge of the sea, a line of white mountains stood against the dark water. Norway, he said, in a voice that sounded older than the boat.

The whales came up beside the ship then. Three of them, longer than the Sea-Hart, slid through the water with a heavy, slow rhythm. They blew clouds of breath that hung in the air. The smell of fish was thick and wet, and it stuck to the wool of their coats. Muninn ruffled its black feathers and did not look up. It had seen whales before, and it did not say so. Ketil woke from a sleep on the deck. He saw the mountains, and he stood very still. His hands gripped the rigging until the knuckles turned white. The ship moved on, and the sea was only the sea.
