The dark lifted slowly, gray and reluctant. Wind tugged at the rigging with a sound like tearing canvas. Spray hit Elsie’s face, cold and sharp, stinging her eyes. She wiped it away with a sleeve, leaving a streak of salt across her cheek. Muninn shifted on the mast, his black feathers ruffling in the gust. He let out a low croak, a sound that matched the groan of the wood. Three shapes breached the water beside the ship, long and gray, breaking the surface with slow, deliberate power. They were longer than the Sea-Hart itself, their bodies rolling as they moved.

Clouds of breath burst from them, thick and white, hanging in the freezing air. The smell was heavy, a stench of old fish and cold brine that settled on their tongues. Grim did not look up from his oar, but he nodded once, a small motion against the grain of the wood. "Good sign," he said. His voice was raspy, scraped thin by the night wind. Elsie watched the whales sink back into the deep, leaving only ripples behind. The gray light strengthened, pushing the shadows back into the water.

The sun rose clear, a pale disc above the horizon. It was the first clear rise Elsie had seen since the frozen field in York. The light hit the deck, warming the wet wood. Ketil sat up, blinking, his hair plastered to his forehead by sweat and salt. He looked past Elsie, past Grim, toward the east. His eyes went wide. A line of white mountains stood on the edge of the sea, jagged and high. Norway. The boy stood up, his legs stiff from sleeping on the cold planks. He stood very still, staring at the distant peaks. The ship rocked gently beneath his feet.
