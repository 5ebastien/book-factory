The long hall smelled of wet wool and woodsmoke, and the fire popped softly in the corner. Nobody slept on the straw, though the night was cold and the river dark outside. Bard stood by the wall, his broad shoulders blocking the window, while he waited for the silence to break. The news had come with a boy on a pony, and it hung in the air like ash.

Ketil sat near the flames, his knees pulled up to his chest, and he watched the embers glow. He told Elsie, very quietly, that he had never been in a fight and did not want to start now. His voice was small, and it disappeared into the smoke. Muninn settled on a beam above, and his eye was hard and dark. He did not speak, but he looked at the door as if it had already closed.

Bard turned from the wall and his eyes met Elsie’s for a second. He said the Sea-Hart would sail when the fighting was over, whoever won it. The words were flat, and they sounded like a stone dropped in a well. Ketil shook his head, and he said he wanted to hide until the morning was past. They did not agree, and the fire crackled on. Elsie stood between them, holding the sunstone in her pocket, and the cold grew against her leg.
