The long hall by the river held no sleep that night. Smoke hung low in the rafters, mixing with the smell of wet wool and fear. A boy on a pony had ridden in with news, his breath steaming in the dark. The Northumbrians were gathered in the hills. Both their kings stood together, ready to take the town back at first light.

Bard stood near the door, his broad shoulders blocking the cold draft. He spoke in a flat, practical voice that cut through the silence. "The Sea-Hart sails when the fighting is over," he said. "Whoever wins, we leave. That is all."

No one argued. The crew stared into the fire, their faces flickering in orange and shadow. Muninn sat on a beam above the flame. The raven did not speak. He only watched, his black eye reflecting the dancing sparks. Elsie sat on a bench, her hands wrapped around a cup of thin broth. It had gone cold, but she held it anyway.

Ketil sat beside her, close enough that their shoulders touched. He was shaking, a small tremor that ran through his thin frame. He looked at the fire, then at Elsie. His voice was barely a whisper. "I have never been in a fight," he said. "I do not want to start now."

He pulled his knees up to his chest, tucking his head in. The weight of the moment pressed down on the room. Bard watched the smoke, waiting for a sign from the sky that would never come. The fire popped and crackled, sending a few embers drifting onto the stone floor.

Muninn stretched his neck. The raven’s eyes flashed with a memory of grey wings against a white sky. Then he burst into the air. He beat his wings once, hard, and dove toward the open door. The cold night air rushed in, chilling Elsie’s face. Muninn shot out into the darkness. He was gone before the firelight reached him.
