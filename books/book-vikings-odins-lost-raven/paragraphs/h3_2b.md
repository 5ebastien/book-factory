The mud in the market smelled of broken eggs and wet wool. Stalls lay pushed over, their wooden frames splintered against the ground. A dog ate from a spilled basket, its muzzle yellow with yolk. Elsie pressed flat against the timber of a stall, her trainers slipping in the muck. Beside her, Ketil held his breath, his face pale and tight with fear. They lay there while the world grew quiet, waiting for the sound of running feet.

From the far end of the market, a line of men with shields went past at a run. Their breath was loud, a heavy fog that hung in the cold air. The thunder of their boots faded, and the only sound left was the wet crunch of the dog chewing. Elsie let out a slow breath, her fingers digging into the dirt. In the mud, just below her hand, a half of a silver coin glinted. It had curly letters on it, strange and dark against the brown sludge.

Muninn looked at the metal, his black eyes wide and still. He remembered a city of white domes, far to the south, where the sun never felt so cold. He remembered hands cutting the silver with a knife, weighing the small pieces on folding scales. Elsie looked at the coin, then at the muddy ground around it. She did not move, only watched the mud settle around the edge of the metal. The dog growled softly, keeping its eyes on the last broken egg.
