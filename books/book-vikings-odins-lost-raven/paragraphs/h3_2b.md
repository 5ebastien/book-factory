The market was a ruin of mud and silence. Stalls lay pushed over, their canvas skins flapping like dead things. A whole basket of eggs lay broken in the slush. A thin, mangy dog stood over the mess, eating the whites. Elsie and Ketil lay flat behind an overturned stand. They held their breath until their lungs burned. Ketil’s elbow rested on Elsie’s ribcage. The pain was a dull, thumping ache. On the far side, the noise changed. A line of men with shields came running past. Their feet slapped the wet ground in a steady rhythm. Shields bumped against each other with sharp, wooden cracks. Elsie pressed her cheek into the cold mud. Ketil went rigid beside her. The line of men roared out of earshot. The sound faded into a distant rumble. They did not move. A cut half of a silver coin lay in the mud by Elsie’s hand. The metal was dull and heavy with dirt. It had curly letters stamped on one side. Muninn landed on the stall edge next to them. The raven looked at the broken piece. Its black eye was deep and still. It remembered a city of domes, very far to the south. The air there had smelled of smoke and cinnamon. That is where this metal had been made. People cut these coins up and weighed the bits. They did not count them; they measured their worth. Elsie looked at the small, curved fragment. She did not reach for it. The dog finished the eggs and trotted away. Ketil let out a long, shuddering breath. Elsie watched the mud ripple with the last drops of rain. The broken silver lay still in the grey.
