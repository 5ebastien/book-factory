Stonegate was a tunnel of hanging signs that swung over the narrow pavement. The raven led the way, its wings folding tight against its body. Elsie ran until her breath burned in her chest. She caught up with it at the corner of a shoe shop. The bird tilted its head and spoke. It remembered a hall with a roof of shields. The smell of wet wool and smoke filled the air. Elsie kept moving. Up the cobbles, past a bakery with a cooling rack in the window. The raven stopped again. This time, it spoke of a man on a high seat. The man had one eye. It was still and cold in the memory. Then, a wolf running under the sun. The sound was like tearing cloth. Elsie’s hands shook where she held her bag. At the top of the street, the Minster stood up grey. It filled the whole space between the buildings. The raven settled on a lamp bracket. It watched her with bright, black eyes. It did not move. Elsie’s phone buzzed in her pocket. It vibrated against her thigh. Grandad’s name flashed on the screen. For the third time. "We need to find the finds officer," Elsie said out loud. Her voice sounded thin in the quiet street. "No," she told herself, looking at the bird. "You go home first." The phone buzzed again, shaking her leg. The raven stared, waiting.
