The street narrowed as the raven hopped from bracket to bracket, each wingbeat rustling against the cold air. Signs of old shops hung overhead, swinging gently in the thin wind, but no wind moved the leaves on the bare trees above. Elsie ran up the cobbles, her trainers slipping slightly on the wet stone, while the bird remained just out of reach. It turned its head sharply, one black eye catching the light, and a memory unspooled from its throat. A hall with a roof of shields was there, bright and red, before the image dissolved into smoke.

At the top of the street, the Minster stood up grey against the sky, its stone face silent and vast. The raven settled on a lamp bracket and waited, feathers ruffling once. Elsie’s phone buzzed in her pocket, then again, and a third time, the screen lighting up with Grandad’s name. She stopped, chest heaving, and looked at the stone in her jacket pocket, where it hummed faintly against her ribs.

"He says to go back to the bench," she said aloud, though the street was empty of any but her own shadow. The raven tilted its head, and another memory surfaced, quiet and sharp. A man on a high seat with one eye looked down, his gaze heavy and still. The phone buzzed again, urgent and close. "He says it’s dangerous out here," Elsie whispered, the words thin in the cold. She did not move, her hand resting on the stone, while the raven stared.
