The branch was wider than a road, stretching out into the grey distance. The bark beneath Elsie’s trainers felt like a cliff face, rough and cold and endless. Dew dripped from the leaves above, thick and slow, like honey falling onto stone. She looked up, but the trunk did not end; it rose beyond sight into the dim light. It went down, too, far below where the air began to hum with a low, heavy weight. The light around her was the colour of dusk that never finished, a flat and featureless grey.

Muninn stood on the branch before her, feathers ruffled by a wind that did not seem to move. The raven opened its beak and spoke, not with a sound, but with a memory of a name. Yggdrasil, it said, as if remembering an old friend long lost in the fog. Every world was on this tree, or under it, held in the vast and silent architecture of the roots.

Far below, a deep chewing sound began, slow and rhythmic, like a giant grinding bone. Elsie shivered, though the air was not cold, only still. Above the canopy, something enormous shifted, and the branches shivered in response. The movement was too big to see, but the weight of it pressed down on the air. She looked at the ground, then at the sky, and felt the branch sway under her feet.
