Elsie was close by the west door, the stone heavy against her chest. The howl arrived not from outside, but at the threshold. It was a low, grinding sound that vibrated in her bones.

Something huge and grey pressed its nose against the wood. The impact shuddered through the floor planks. The carved wolf over the door frame seemed to shrink in the shadow of the beast. It looked like a joke made of chisel marks now, a tiny thing against the bulk of the real thing.

Muninn ruffled his feathers on Elsie’s shoulder. He said nothing. He only watched the wood bow outward with a groan.

The warriors nearest the door stood up. Their movements were slow and deliberate, heavy with muscle and age. Hands reached for shields resting against the pillars. The metal rings clinked softly in the sudden quiet.

Sköll had followed the stone all the way here. The air smelled of pine needles and wet fur. It was a cold smell, biting at the back of the throat.

The wood creaked. A split appeared, thin as a thread, running down the grain. Dust puffed from the crack. The wolf’s breath fogged the gap, a white cloud that vanished instantly in the warm hall air.

Elsie watched the split widen. The carved wolf’s eye, once fierce, now looked pitifully small. The grain of the oak began to splinter. A long, grey shape moved in the darkness beyond the door. It was waiting.

The wind from the crack was sharp and clean. It smelled of nothing but ice. The stone at Elsie’s neck pulsed once, cold against her skin.
