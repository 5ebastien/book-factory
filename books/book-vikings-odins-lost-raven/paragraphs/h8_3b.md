The howl came again, and this time it was not far off. It was at the doors. Sköll had followed the stone all the way here. The warriors nearest the west door stood up and reached for their shields. Something huge and grey pressed its nose against the wood. The carved wolf above seemed to shrink back into the stone.

Elsie felt the cold air shift in the hall. The smell of wet fur mixed with the old scent of smoke. Muninn ruffled his feathers on her shoulder. His eyes were wide and dark. He looked at the door, then at Elsie. He did not make a sound. He only looked at the door.

The wooden planks creaked under the weight of the beast. Dust fell from the rafters of spears. The roof of shields rattled softly. The great grey eye opened, yellow and blind. It stared through the wood as if the barrier did not exist. Elsie’s heart beat hard against her ribs. The warriors held their ground. No one moved. No one spoke.

Then the nose pushed harder. A deep crack split the air. The west door groaned. The carved wolf seemed to vanish. Only the raw wood remained.
