The oar dipped into the cold water and rose again, a steady rhythm that matched the song. The rowers sang about a woman who married a troll, their voices low and dragging through the mist. Elsie sat on the wooden sea chest, her thighs aching from the damp wood that pressed against her jeans. Her hands were raw now, the red lines of the oar handle cutting into her palms with every stroke. The river wound through flat, wet country where the grass grew thick and dark and the sky sat low and heavy. Nobody was in the fields; only the grey water moved around the keel of the Sea-Hart, slow and deep. Muninn sat on the mast crossbar, watching the water with one eye that seemed to hold the past. The town’s smoke was just a smudge behind them, fading into the afternoon air until it was gone. Ketil sat near the front, his face pale as he looked at the empty landscape. Bard did not speak, only watching the line of the river as it bent and turned away from the known land. The light began to fail, turning the water from silver to a dull, brown mud. The river grew wider and the current pulled at the boat, smoother and faster than before. As the air turned cold and the sun dropped below the trees, the wide mouth of the Humber opened up ahead of them. Elsie felt the cold settle in her bones while the song of the troll woman faded into the silence.
