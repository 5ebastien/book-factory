The howl did not come from one direction but from the air itself, a low vibration that shivered the grey leaves of the Tree. Muninn ruffled its feathers and spoke in a small, thin voice, remembering a frozen field from eleven hundred years ago. It recalled the morning it was lost, standing in the frost while that same terrible sound rolled over the ground. Elsie clutched the sunstone, feeling it burn against her palm, hotter than it had ever been before.

The heat spread through her fingers, and she knew, with a cold certainty, that Sköll had found her scent. The wind died down, leaving a silence that pressed against her ears, too quiet for a place so large. Suddenly, a second shadow detached from the branches above, dropping down with a sharp, restless beat of wings. This raven was the same size as Muninn but moved like a quick thought, its eyes sharp and unsteady.

Huginn landed on a low branch and tilted its head, asking if she intended to stay and wait for the wolf. The question hung in the still air, unanswered, while the stone continued to pulse with its strange, growing warmth. Elsie looked at the two birds, then down at her trembling hands, the stone a heavy black weight in her grip.
