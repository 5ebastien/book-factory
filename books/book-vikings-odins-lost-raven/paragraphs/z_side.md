The air under the wall was thick and cold, smelling of damp wood and old smoke. Elsie kept her back against the rough timber, feeling the vibrations of the wolf’s heavy paws travel through the floorboards. Muninn pressed his head against her chest, his feathers cold and tight in her coat. The firelight from the center of the hall flickered, casting long, swinging shadows that danced over the hanging shields. Those shields clinked softly together, a sound like bones knocking in the dark.

She watched the space between the legs, where the light did not reach. Sköll moved with a slow, heavy confidence, his grey fur matted with dust from the road. He sniffed the air, and the scent of iron and ozone rose up from the stone in Elsie’s fist. Her knuckles turned white as she squeezed harder, until the edges cut into her palm. The wolf stopped. Elsie stopped too, holding her breath until her lungs burned.

For a long moment, the only sound was the tick of the cooling fires. Then, a low growl rumbled from deep in Sköll’s chest, a sound that seemed to shake the very beams overhead. He shifted his weight, his yellow eyes scanning the dark corners just beyond the light. Elsie waited, her heart hammering against the small, warm body of the raven. The shadows stretched long and thin, pointing toward the high seat at the far end. There, the steps waited, empty and silent.
