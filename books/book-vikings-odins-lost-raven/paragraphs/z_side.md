The air under the shields was cold and smelled of wet wool. Elsie pressed her back against the rough wood of the long table. She could feel the vibration of Sköll’s paws on the floorboards. Inside her coat, Muninn was a heavy, warm lump against her ribs. The raven did not move. He waited with the patience of something that had been waiting for a very long time. The wolf moved through the center of the hall, close to the line of firelight. He sniffed the air, his nose twitching at every change in the scent. The heat from the fires bent away from him, curling into the shadows he passed through. Elsie held her breath until her lungs burned. Sköll stopped. His head swiveled slowly to the left, ears flat against his skull. Elsie did not breathe. She watched a single drop of melted wax fall from a nearby torch. It hit the floor with a tiny, sharp pop. The wolf’s ears flicked. He did not turn. He resumed his pace, slow and deliberate, sure that his quarry was ahead of him. Elsie exhaled, a thin stream of white mist in the chill air. She moved again, step by step, her trainers silent on the packed earth. The hanging shields clattered softly as the air moved around them. Each one was a shadow on top of another shadow. She kept her fist tight around the sunstone. It was cold and hard, pressing against her palm like a small, angry star. At the end of the wall, the path ended. The high seat stood before her, occupied by a dark shape in a wide hat. The steps up to it were smooth from centuries of use. There, at the top, was the one-eyed man. He waited, his head tilted slightly to the side, as if listening to a sound only he could hear.
