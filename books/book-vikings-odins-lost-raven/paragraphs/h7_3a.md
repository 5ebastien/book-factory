The wind moved through the gold leaves, and a low ringing filled the air; it sounded like glass being rubbed by dry fingers. Elsie pressed her gloved hands to her ears, but the noise did not stop. The light here was thick and warm, even though the air remained sharp and cold against her cheeks.

Muninn landed on her shoulder, and he pecked at her ear with a beak that felt like cold stone. He spoke of a hall where shields were nailed to the roof, a roof so wide it went on further than any building should. Elsie wanted to lie down under the trees and look up at the gold forever, but the raven bit her again.

She looked past the trunks, where the wood thinned and the ground sloped down toward a structure. The rafters were made of spears, and the roof was a quilt of dull, red-painted shields. Smoke rose from fifty smoke holes, drifting straight up into the high, clear air.

There were windows in the walls, and through them, she saw figures moving in slow, heavy lines. They stood in rows, holding drinking horns that glowed with amber liquid. The hall stood before the end of the wood, and every door in it was painted green.

Elsie reached out to touch one of the leaves, and it rang out clear and high under her fingertip.
