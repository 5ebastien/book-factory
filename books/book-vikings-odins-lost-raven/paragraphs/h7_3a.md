The leaves were gold, every single one of them. They did not look like real leaves. They looked like hammered metal, thin and bright. When the breeze moved through the branches, the whole forest made a sound like a thousand tiny bells. Elsie stopped walking. She dropped her weight into a squat, her trainers pressing into the dark earth. She wanted to stay here. She wanted to stay here for years. The cold in her padded coat seemed to fade, replaced by a heavy, warm stillness.

Muninn landed on her shoulder. The bird’s claws dug into her shoulder, sharp and cold. She flinched, but she did not move away. The raven turned his head and looked at the trees. His beak snapped open, and a sound came out. It was not a word. It was a picture.

A long hall stood between the trees. It was huge. The roof was made of shields, hundreds of them, catching the light. Inside, figures moved in the shadows. They were holding spears. The sound of the gold leaves continued, a high, ringing tone that vibrated in Elsie’s teeth. She looked closer at the hall. The walls were solid. The doors were numerous, stretching away into the distance.

Muninn ruffled his feathers. He tapped her cheek with one wing. The memory was bright and clear, like a scar that would not fade. Elsie watched the figures inside the hall. They moved with purpose. The gold leaves above them swayed, casting dancing shadows on the ground. She reached out a hand, her fingers trembling slightly. She did not touch the tree. She just hovered her hand near the bark, feeling the heat rise from it. The raven tilted his head, waiting. The bells of the leaves continued to ring.
