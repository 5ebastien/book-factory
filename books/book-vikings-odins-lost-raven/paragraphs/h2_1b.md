The shipyard smelled of sharp tar and wet wood. Oak logs lay on rollers, waiting for the sea. Chips of splintered timber crunched underfoot. The slipway was crowded with men mending hulls. They hammered nails into fresh planks with rhythmic thuds. Every ship here reeked of the black, sticky liquid. It coated their hands and clothes. Elsie stood near the water’s edge, feeling the chill. Muninn sat on her shoulder, feathers tight against the wind. A boy about her age worked nearby. He pushed tarred wool into a wooden seam. His face was grimy, his eyes focused downward. He muttering softly to himself as he worked. He did not look up when Elsie stepped closer. The iron tool in his hand was blunt and heavy. He pressed the wool down, forcing it into place. After a long time, he stopped. His dark eyes drifted up to the raven. He stared at the bird for several seconds. The silence between them stretched thin and cold. "You have a bird," he said finally. His voice was rough, like a stuck hinge. "It looks cold." Elsie touched the stone at her neck instinctively. The glass felt warm against her skin. The boy shifted his weight on the slipway. "I am Ketil," he said, not offering a hand. He looked back at the ship before him. "The Sea-Hart." He nodded toward the vessel’s hull. "She is going home soon." His eyes returned to Muninn. "Where does she go?" Elsie watched his face. He waited for an answer, his hands still stained black with tar. The wind pushed a clump of dried seaweed across the wood. Muninn tilted his head, watching the boy. Ketil leaned forward slightly, resting his elbows on his knees.
