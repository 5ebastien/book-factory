On the slipway, the air was thick with the sharp, burning smell of hot tar. Oak logs leaned against each other on rollers, waiting for the sea. Elsie stood near the edge, where the wet sand stained the hem of her jeans. Muninn sat on her shoulder, his feathers flat and black against the grey morning. The raven did not move, only watched the boats with eyes like polished stone.

Near a large hull, a boy about Elsie’s age was pushing wet wool into a crack. He used a blunt iron tool, muttering low words at the stubborn seam. This was Ketil, the ship’s boy for the vessel called the Sea-Hart. The ship was ready to leave soon, heading north to a fjord he had not seen for two winters. He paused, wiping his forehead, and looked at the bird on Elsie’s shoulder. He looked at it for a long time, his expression shifting from annoyance to quiet curiosity.

Elsie felt the weight of his gaze. She kept her hands in her coat pockets and waited. The boy stepped closer, stepping over a pile of wood chips. He did not ask where the bird came from. Instead, he asked if it knew the way north. Muninn opened his beak, but no sound came out, only a low, vibrating hum. The boy’s face fell, just slightly, as if he had expected an answer. He turned back to the ship and pressed the iron into the wood again. Elsie watched the steam rise from the tar pot nearby. The wind carried the smell of the sea, strong and cold.
