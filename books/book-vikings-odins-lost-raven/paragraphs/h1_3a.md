Grandad descended the worn stone steps, his fingers tight on the cold iron rail, while his old knees complained with every downward motion. At the bottom, the River Ouse had swollen over the edge of the staith, a dark and heavy expanse that swallowed the wooden planks whole. The modern lamps along the bank flickered to life, casting a yellow glow on the wet mud, but the water itself looked wrong. Elsie touched the sunstone at her throat, where it pulsed with a slow and steady warmth against her skin. She raised the clear disc to her eye and looked through it at the grey, churning river below. Through the stone, the concrete embankment and the metal railings simply faded away like mist in the morning sun. In their place stood a muddy bank, bare and ancient, with a row of wooden jetties sticking out into the brown water. Long, low ships were drawn up on the shore, their masts silent and their hulls dark against the dying light. The air smelled of salt and old wood, not of traffic or the city beyond the river. Grandad called her name, but the sound came to her faintly, as if he were standing at the end of a very long and quiet corridor. She lowered the stone from her face, and the modern street lamps flickered again, uncertain and weak. The river still ran high beneath them, dark and cold, but the world felt thinner than before.
