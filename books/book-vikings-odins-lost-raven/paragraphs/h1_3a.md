The water lapped at the bottom step, cold and brown. Elsie looked down through the stone hanging at her chest. The river below vanished. In its place was mud, wide and soft. Wooden jetties jutted out into the still water. Long, low ships sat drawn up on the bank. Their sails were furled tight against the cold air. There were no embankments here. No paved paths. Just the raw edge of the river and the smell of damp wood and old rope. The lamps along the staith flickered. Then they went out, one by one. The darkness pressed in around the stone’s glow. Elsie felt the weight of it against her throat. It was heavy, like a stone pulled from a deep well. She lowered her hand to her neck. The smooth surface was cold to the touch. Grandad was on the step just above her, one hand on the rail. He was close enough to touch, but his voice sounded thin and far away, like a shout across a valley. "Elsie?" he said again. The wind gusted, pushing the mists closer. The older world beneath seemed to breathe in time with it. She looked back up at the real street. The light was gone from the lamps. Only the faint shine of the stone remained. It pulsed softly in the dusk. Elsie gripped the edge of the step. Her trainers slipped slightly on the wet stone. She did not move.
